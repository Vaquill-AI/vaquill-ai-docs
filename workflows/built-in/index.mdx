Twenty-five workflows ship with Vaquill AI. Each one is a maintained pipeline with its own document slots, typed input form, orchestration, and fixed artifact set. You do not configure them; you feed them and collect the output.

They split into two gallery categories, In-House and Litigation, and are further grouped here by the job they do.

## How to read a workflow page

Every page follows the same shape:

<Steps>
  <Step title="What it is for">
    The specific scenario the workflow was built around.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What you need">
    Its document slots: which roles, how many documents each accepts, and whether the slot is required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inputs">
    Every field on the launcher form, its type, whether it is required, its options, and what changing it actually does to the output.
  </Step>
  <Step title="How it runs">
    The pipeline stages, named the same way the live progress view names them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What you get">
    Every artifact, its format, and its contents.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What it will not do">
    The explicit limitations. Read this before you decide the workflow is the right tool.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Two shared pipeline shapes

Most workflows are one of two patterns, which is worth knowing because it tells you how they scale.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Matrix review" icon="table">
    `prepare → extract → wait for extraction → normalize and score → synthesize → render`

    Builds an extraction matrix over the document set, answers every cell, scores each document, then synthesizes across the portfolio and verifies that synthesis. Scales to hundreds of documents.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Drafting" icon="pen-nib">
    `prepare → generate → export`

    Gathers context from the reference documents and your form answers, generates the instrument, and exports DOCX. Fast, single-output, jurisdiction-aware.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The remaining workflows, including the agentic ones and the jurisdiction comparison, have bespoke pipelines documented on their own pages.

## Cost and scale

**Workflow runs are not billed per run.** There is no per-run charge, no credit deduction, and no separate workflow quota. Every workflow here is covered by your subscription, so running M&A Due Diligence across a 200-document data room costs the same as running Document Triage on one file. See [Plans, trial, and limits](/guides/plans-and-limits).

What does vary is **time**, and that is worth planning around.

| Portfolio size | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| 1-10 documents | The low end of the runtime range on each page. Fine to wait for. |
| 10-50 documents | The high end of the range. Launch it and come back. |
| 50-200+ documents | Use a workflow built for it. Six run on a dedicated queue with a two-hour ceiling: Compliance Scan Agent, Matter Triage Agent, Obligations Tracker, Deposition Analyzer, Privilege Log Builder, and M&A Due Diligence. |

Three levers control noise and runtime on large sets:

- **Materiality thresholds.** [M&A Due Diligence](/workflows/built-in/ma-due-diligence) and [Obligations Tracker](/workflows/built-in/obligations-renewal-tracker) summarize below-threshold findings in aggregate instead of itemizing them.
- **Focus areas.** M&A Due Diligence runs five domain specialists by default; picking a subset skips the rest entirely.
- **Regime scoping.** [Compliance Scan Agent](/workflows/built-in/compliance-scan-agent) sweeps every applicable regime unless you narrow it.

<Warning>
  A run in flight when a trial lapses will not finish. Start long-running workflows with time to spare.
</Warning>

## Jurisdiction

Most workflows are US-only. A handful, listed on their own pages, accept any jurisdiction. Nothing in the catalogue is India-specific: the corpus and the drafting defaults are US.
