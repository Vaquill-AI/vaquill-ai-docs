This page states what you are allowed to build with the data, so you do not need to book a call to find out.

Terms used here: **Output** is the data the API returns to you.
The **compilation** is our schema, citations, hierarchy, cross-references, metadata and the assembled corpus.
A **bulk license** is a paid delivery of the corpus under a separate signed agreement.

<Note>
  This page is a plain-language summary.
  The [Terms of Service](https://www.vaquill.ai/terms), Section 21, is what actually governs, and it controls if the two ever disagree.
</Note>

## The short version

| Can I... | Answer | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Cache and persist responses in my own database? | Yes, including permanently, where that store powers your product | Section 21 |
| Build embeddings, vector indexes or other derived indexes? | Yes, for your own product | Section 21 |
| Show the legal text to my own end users? | Yes, with a credit unless your plan waives it | Sections 21 and 24 |
| Keep what I cached from the API if I stop paying? | Yes. No deletion obligation | Section 21 |
| Keep a bulk license delivery after the contract ends? | No. Delete the compilation within 30 days | Section 21 |
| Redistribute the statutory text itself? | Yes, without restriction. It is public domain | Not restricted |
| Resell the structured corpus as a dataset, feed or API? | No, not without a separate signed agreement | Section 21.7 |
| Ship it under my own brand? | Under a white-label agreement, yes | Section 21.7 |
| Train a model on it? | Yes, unless the model is a competing legal-data service | Section 21 |

<Accordion title="Why the text is unrestricted">
  The statutory, regulatory and constitutional text is US primary law, and US primary law cannot be copyrighted.
  *Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, Inc.*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020) settled it, holding that even annotations prepared under contract for a legislature are uncopyrightable government edicts.

  We claim no ownership of that text and nothing in our terms restricts your use of it.
  The public-domain text carries no restriction either way, and never did.

  What is licensed to you is the compilation: the schema, the citations, the hierarchy, the cross-references, the metadata and the assembled corpus.
  That is the work product, and the restrictions below apply to it alone.
</Accordion>

## What you keep if you leave

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Retrieved through the API">
    Responses you cached during a paid term are yours to keep.
    There is no deletion obligation, and you may keep showing them to your customers.
    That includes the citations, hierarchy and cross-references those responses carry, because a cached answer is not usable without them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Delivered in bulk">
    The rule differs here on purpose.
    On termination or expiry of a bulk license you delete stored copies of our compilation within 30 days.
    The corpus itself is the deliverable, so permanent retention would let a single period buy it outright.

    You still keep anything you had independently retrieved through the API, the public-domain text, anything obtained under the Open US Law licenses, and Output already embedded in work product you delivered to your clients.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Retention is not a license to redistribute">
    Whatever you keep, you may not resell our compilation as a standalone data product, nor use it to build a competing legal-data API or database.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What you may not do

Without a separate signed agreement, you will not:

- systematically extract, bulk-download, scrape or accumulate the corpus for any purpose other than powering your own end-user product
- resell, sublicense or redistribute the underlying legal data or our compilation as a standalone dataset, database, feed or API
- offer the API, or a service substantially derived from it, under your own or a third party's brand
- use the API or its Output to build, train or improve a product that competes with our legal-research, statute-retrieval or legal-data API services
- reverse engineer the API or attempt to derive its models or training data
- exceed or circumvent your plan's rate limits or credit allowance, including by spreading access across accounts

Each of those is available under a written agreement, so they are commercial terms and not matters of principle.

## Multiple clients and end users

You can create several API keys per account, and every request is recorded against the key that made it, so usage can be reported per key.
One key per client is an easy way to keep each client's usage separate.
If you'd rather have full separation, each client can have its own organization, with its own keys and billing.

If you build your own product on the API and serve your own end users, [Section 24 of the Terms](https://vaquill.ai/terms) covers that.
You hold the relationship with each end customer, and they don't need Vaquill AI accounts.
Reselling or white-labelling our data as a standalone dataset or API is different and needs a separate written agreement (Section 21.7).
For what the API stores per key and per client, see [Data and privacy](/api-guide/data-privacy).

## Attribution

Unless your plan waives it, display **"Legal data by Vaquill AI"**, linked to vaquill.ai, somewhere a user can reasonably find it in any product where the Output is shown to your end users.
A footer, an about page or a data-sources page is fine.
One credit per product, not one per response.
The console gives you the exact snippet.

It is not required for internal or back-office use where nothing is shown outside your organization, or for evaluation.

Attribution can be waived commercially. Talk to us.

What you retrieve through the API is covered by the Terms, Section 21, not by these licenses.
The snapshot grants, including what the CC BY 4.0 snapshots allow, are on [Bulk and SQL access](/api-guide/bulk-and-sql#license).
