---
title: "Point-in-time law"
description: "Get the text of a US provision as it read on a past date."
---

Ask what a provision said on a date, not just what it says now.

```bash
curl "https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/section/STATE_ID_IDAPA_16_03_21_010/body?asOf=2026-06-01" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."
```

`asOf=YYYY-MM-DD` on `GET /us/statutes/section/{actId}/body` returns the section's text as it stood on that date.
It is billed as an ordinary body fetch.

This matters when the question is dated: what an employer's obligation was on the day of the conduct, which version of a rule a contract signed in 2019 incorporated, what a regulation required before an amendment.
Today's text gives the wrong answer to all of these.

## Choosing the right tool

| Question | Use |
|---|---|
| What did this section say on a date? | `asOf` on `/section/{actId}/body` |
| What changed, and when? | [`/section/{actId}/changes`](/api-guide/concepts/section-intelligence) |
| Give me the whole 2004 CFR as printed | `corpusType=CFR_ANNUAL` |
| Tell me when it changes from now on | [Law change alerts](/api-guide/alerts) |
| Which sections moved since my last sync? | `changedSince` on `/statutes/search` |

## Every answer says where it came from

The response carries an `asOf` block.
Read `engine` first, then `source`: the same parameter is answered by two different mechanisms, and one of them is an estimate.

| `engine` | `source` | What you are holding | Charged |
|---|---|---|---|
| `observed_change` | `live` | We captured no change after your date, so today's text is what stood then, as far as we ever saw. | Yes |
| `observed_change` | `reconstructed` | Rebuilt from the before-side of the first change we captured after your date. | Yes |
| `observed_change` | `unavailable` | We know it changed but cannot rebuild the earlier text. No text is returned. | No, refunded |
| `stored_edition` | `published` | An edition we hold answered, verbatim. It is the publisher's own text, with no estimate from us. | Yes |
| `stored_edition` | `unavailable` | No edition we hold governs your date. No text is returned. | No, refunded |

`observed_change` answers every corpus that does not hold published editions.
The text is rebuilt from change history we observed, and reaches back only as far as capture began for that source.

`stored_edition` answers from a publisher's own dated edition, held verbatim: `CFR_ANNUAL`, the U.S. Code, the Copyright Compendium and the OFAC FAQs.

On `stored_edition`, `outOfCoverageReason` says why no text came back in a sentence, and names the earliest edition we hold when your date predates it.
The editions are enumerated, not interpolated: a date between two of them, or before the first, is reported out of coverage rather than answered from a neighbouring edition, because the text may have changed in the gap.

A fourth outcome is not a `source` value at all: on `observed_change`, `existed: false` means the earliest thing we ever captured for this section is its addition, and that addition postdates your date, so it demonstrably did not exist yet.
That is a definite answer, so it is charged.
On `stored_edition` with `source: "unavailable"`, `existed` carries no meaning and is always `false`: read `source` and `outOfCoverageReason`.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `requested` | The date you asked for, echoed back. |
| `existed` | On `observed_change`, false when the section demonstrably did not exist yet on your date. |
| `basisChangeId` | The change event whose before-side supplied this text. Null unless `source` is `reconstructed`. |
| `observedFrom` | The earliest change we ever captured for this section. |
| `isBounded` | Means different things on the two engines. On `observed_change`, true marks a bounded reconstruction: see the warning below. On `stored_edition`, true means your date falls inside an edition we hold, so the text is a verified published printing, and false means out of coverage with no text. |
| `coverage` | The same limit in prose, safe to show a reader verbatim. |
| `outOfCoverageReason` | `stored_edition` only. Why no text came back, when `source` is `unavailable`. |
| `resolvedEditionYear`, `scheduledEditionYear`, `editionsObserved` | `CFR_ANNUAL` only. The edition year actually served, the year the revision schedule computed, and the editions held for the section. |
| `html` | Always null on an `asOf` answer, on every corpus. Reconstructed text is plain text, and we do not synthesize markup the publisher never printed. Read `plain`, as on every body response. |

`engine` and `source` are in the table above.

To see the event a `reconstructed` answer rests on, read [`GET /us/statutes/section/{actId}/changes`](/api-guide/concepts/section-intelligence) with `sinceId` set one below `basisChangeId` and `order=asc`.

<Warning>
  On `observed_change`, `isBounded: true` means we observed no change affecting that date.
  It does not mean there was none.

  It is set when your date predates `observedFrom`, or when we captured no change for that section at all.
  Change capture began long after the corpus did, and runs per source, so a bounded answer is our best reconstruction rather than a verified historical text.

  Surface it.
  Rendering a bounded answer as authoritative point-in-time law makes a claim we did not make.
</Warning>

## How each corpus answers

`asOf` resolves two different ways depending on the corpus, and the difference decides how much weight the answer carries.
Where the publisher issues dated editions, the answer is the edition in force on your date.
Everywhere else the corpus holds one current text per citation, and the earlier one is rebuilt from observed change history: accurate to what we captured, bounded by when capture began.

| Corpus | How | Point-in-time behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `CFR_ANNUAL` | Edition | 31 GPO annual editions, 1996-2026, all 49 titles. `asOf` resolves to the edition in force. |
| `USC` | Edition | Two editions: the one current through 2025-01-06 (served only for that date) and the current release point, which governs from 2026-09-02. Every date in between, and every earlier date, is out of coverage and returns no text. |
| `FEDERAL_RULES` | Edition | 1,125 superseded texts of 510 Federal Rules, each its own `FEDERAL_RULES_ANNUAL` document (`{current actId}_E{year}`) built from GPO's annual US Code appendix editions. Pass the rule's current `actId` and `asOf` resolves to the text in force. The record begins 1998-01-26. |
| `copyright_compendium` | Edition | Three dated releases (2014, 2017, 2021). Resolves to the edition the Copyright Office states applied. |
| `ofac_faq` | Separate document | 361 prior FAQ versions are separate citable documents from OFAC's own archive, served `actStatus: "superseded"`. |
| `whd_opinion_letter` | Separate document | Withdrawn and superseded letters stay retrievable under their own `act_id`, including the 2009 letters WHD re-issued in 2018 under new numbers. |
| CMS Medicare manuals, DOL Field Operations Handbook | Capture only | The publisher replaces text in place and issues no superseded editions, so there is nothing to reconstruct from beyond our own capture history. Expect `isBounded: true` on dates before capture began. |

Where history is a separate document, you retrieve it by citation instead of by date.

<Accordion title="Edge cases by corpus">
On `CFR_ANNUAL`, `editionsObserved` is an enumeration and never a range: a version observed in 1998, 1999 and 2003 lists exactly those years, and a date in an unobserved gap returns `asOf.isBounded: false` rather than the nearest year held.
Also on `CFR_ANNUAL`, a citation year is the volume's own `<REVISED>` year, which can differ from the folder GPO filed it under, because GPO reprints unchanged volumes forward without re-dating them.

On a Federal Rule, a date before 1998-01-26 returns the earliest text we hold with `asOf.isBounded: true`, which is not the text in force on that date.
A date before the rule existed answers that it did not exist.
So does a date after a rule was deleted and before its number was reused: Criminal Rule 59 answers that it did not exist between 2002-12-01 and 2005-12-01.
When a later edition corrected a misprint without a new amendment, the corrected printing is served for every date that amendment governs.
Editions were not published for every year, so a text in force only between two editions we hold can be missing.
Past texts are also citable on their own under `corpusType=FEDERAL_RULES_ANNUAL`, which is opt-in and never part of an unscoped search.

On `whd_opinion_letter`, a re-issued pair shares one PDF at the publisher but is two documents with opposite status.
</Accordion>

## Worked example

The request at the top of this page asks what an Idaho rule said on 1 June 2026.
The response:

```json
{
  "actId": "STATE_ID_IDAPA_16_03_21_010",
  "html": null,
  "plain": "...",
  "available": true,
  "asOf": {
    "requested": "2026-06-01",
    "engine": "observed_change",
    "source": "reconstructed",
    "existed": true,
    "basisChangeId": 180183,
    "observedFrom": "2026-10-26T05:10:38.805852+00:00",
    "isBounded": true,
    "coverage": "Observed changes only: what a refresh of this source detected, from when change capture began for it through today, retained 5 years. ..."
  }
}
```

`isBounded` is true here because 2026-06-01 predates `observedFrom`.
The text is our best reconstruction, and a product built on it should say so to its reader.

<Accordion title="A date no held edition governs">
A U.S. Code date that no held edition governs answers like this instead, with no text and no charge:

```json
{
  "actId": "USC_T29_C8_S207",
  "plain": null,
  "available": false,
  "asOf": {
    "requested": "2019-06-01",
    "engine": "stored_edition",
    "source": "unavailable",
    "existed": false,
    "isBounded": false,
    "outOfCoverageReason": "We hold no published edition governing 2019-06-01, so the text in force on that date is not served. The earliest edition we hold is dated 2025-01-06, which is after your date."
  }
}
```
</Accordion>
