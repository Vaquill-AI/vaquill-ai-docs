---
title: "Point-in-time law"
description: "Retrieve US statutes, regulations and agency guidance as they read on a past date, with provenance saying whether the text is a published edition, a reconstruction, or unavailable"
---

Ask what a provision said on a date, not just what it says now.

```bash
curl "https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/section/STATE_ID_IDAPA_16_03_21_010/body?asOf=2026-06-01" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."
```

`asOf=YYYY-MM-DD` on `GET /us/statutes/section/{actId}/body` returns the section's text as it stood
on that date. It is billed as an ordinary body fetch.

This matters when the question is dated: what an employer's obligation was on the day of the
conduct, which version of a rule a contract signed in 2019 incorporated, what a regulation required
before an amendment. Today's text gives the wrong answer to all of these.

## Every answer says where it came from

The response carries an `asOf` block.
Read `engine` first, then `source`, because the same parameter is answered by two different mechanisms and one of them is an estimate.

| `engine` | What answered |
|---|---|
| `observed_change` | Every corpus that does not hold published editions. The text is rebuilt from change history we observed, and reaches back only as far as capture began for that source. |
| `stored_edition` | A publisher's own dated edition, held verbatim: `CFR_ANNUAL`, the U.S. Code, the Copyright Compendium and the OFAC FAQs. |

Within `observed_change`, `source` is one of:

| `source` | What you are holding | Charged |
|---|---|---|
| `live` | We captured no change after your date, so today's text is what stood then, as far as we ever saw. | Yes |
| `reconstructed` | Rebuilt from the before-side of the first change we captured after your date. | Yes |
| `unavailable` | We know it changed but cannot rebuild the earlier text. No text is returned. | No, refunded |

Within `stored_edition`, `source` is `published` (an edition we hold answered, verbatim, charged) or `unavailable` (no edition we hold governs your date, no text is returned, refunded).
`outOfCoverageReason` says why in a sentence, and names the earliest edition we hold when your date predates it.
The editions are enumerated, not interpolated: a date between two of them, or before the first, is reported out of coverage rather than answered from a neighbouring edition, because the text may have changed in the gap.

A fourth outcome is not a `source` value at all: on `observed_change`, `existed: false` means the earliest thing we ever
captured for this section is its addition, and that addition postdates your date, so it
demonstrably did not exist yet. That is a definite answer, so it is charged.
On `stored_edition` with `source: "unavailable"`, `existed` carries no meaning and is always `false`: read `source` and `outOfCoverageReason`.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `requested` | The date you asked for, echoed back. |
| `engine` | `observed_change` or `stored_edition`. |
| `source` | `live`, `reconstructed`, `published` or `unavailable`. |
| `existed` | On `observed_change`, false when the section demonstrably did not exist yet on your date. |
| `basisChangeId` | The change event whose before-side supplied this text. Null unless `source` is `reconstructed`. |
| `observedFrom` | The earliest change we ever captured for this section. |
| `isBounded` | The flag means different things on the two engines. On `observed_change`, true means we observed no change affecting that date, which is not the same as there having been none. On `stored_edition`, true means your date falls inside an edition we hold, so the text is a verified published printing, and false means out of coverage with no text. |
| `coverage` | The same limit in prose, safe to show a reader verbatim. |
| `outOfCoverageReason` | `stored_edition` only. Why no text came back, when `source` is `unavailable`. |
| `resolvedEditionYear`, `scheduledEditionYear`, `editionsObserved` | `CFR_ANNUAL` only. The edition year actually served, the year the revision schedule computed, and the editions held for the section. |

The text is in `plain`, as on every body response, and `html` is always null on an `asOf` answer.

To see the event a `reconstructed` answer rests on, read
[`GET /us/statutes/section/{actId}/changes`](/api-guide/concepts/section-intelligence) with
`sinceId` set one below `basisChangeId` and `order=asc`.

<Warning>
  On `observed_change`, `isBounded: true` means we observed no change affecting that date. It does not mean
  there was none.

  It is set when your date predates `observedFrom`, or when we captured no change for that section
  at all. Change capture began long after the corpus did, and runs per source, so a bounded answer
  is our best reconstruction rather than a verified historical text.

  Surface it. Rendering a bounded answer as authoritative point-in-time law makes a claim we did
  not make.
</Warning>

<Note>
  An `asOf` response never populates `html`, on any corpus. Reconstructed text is plain text, and
  synthesizing markup the publisher never printed would defeat the point of the endpoint. Use
  `text`, or `content` where the corpus supports it.
</Note>

## Reconstruction versus a published edition

`asOf` resolves two different ways depending on the corpus, and the difference decides how much
weight the answer carries.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="A published edition">
    For corpora where the publisher issues dated editions, `asOf` resolves to the edition that
    was in force on your date, served as published. It is the publisher's own text, with no
    estimate from us.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A reconstruction">
    Everywhere else, the corpus holds one current text per citation and the earlier one is rebuilt
    from observed change history. Accurate to what we captured, bounded by when capture began.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Corpora that answer from a published edition

| Corpus | How point-in-time works |
|---|---|
| `CFR_ANNUAL` | 31 GPO annual editions, 1996-2026, all 49 titles. `asOf` resolves to the edition in force. |
| `USC` | Two editions: the one current through 2025-01-06 (served only for that date) and the current release point, which governs from 2026-09-02. Every date in between, and every earlier date, is out of coverage and returns no text. |
| `copyright_compendium` | Three dated releases (2014, 2017, 2021). Resolves to the edition the Copyright Office states applied. |
| `ofac_faq` | 361 prior FAQ versions are separate citable documents from OFAC's own archive, served `actStatus: "superseded"`. |

Two behaviors to expect on `CFR_ANNUAL`. First, `editionsObserved` is an enumeration and never a
range: a version observed in 1998, 1999 and 2003 lists exactly those years, and a date in an
unobserved gap returns `asOf.isBounded: false` rather than the nearest year held. Second, a
citation year is the volume's own `<REVISED>` year, which can differ from the folder GPO filed it
under, because GPO reprints unchanged volumes forward without re-dating them.

### Where history is a separate document rather than a date

Some corpora publish their own prior versions, so you retrieve history by citation instead of by
date. `whd_opinion_letter` keeps withdrawn and superseded letters retrievable under their own
`act_id`, including the 2009 letters WHD re-issued in 2018 under new numbers. A re-issued pair
shares one PDF at the publisher but is two documents with opposite status.

### Where point-in-time is weakest

Corpora whose publisher replaces text in place and issues no superseded editions, such as the CMS
Medicare manuals and the DOL Field Operations Handbook, give us nothing to reconstruct from beyond
our own capture history. Expect `isBounded: true` on dates before capture began.

## Worked example

```bash
# What did this Idaho rule say on 1 June 2026, before the October 2026 refresh amended it?
curl "https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/section/STATE_ID_IDAPA_16_03_21_010/body?asOf=2026-06-01" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."
```

```json
{
  "actId": "STATE_ID_IDAPA_16_03_21_010",
  "html": null,
  "plain": "...",
  "available": true,
  "asOf": {
    "requested": "2026-06-01",
    "engine": "observed_change",
    "source": "reconstructed",
    "existed": true,
    "basisChangeId": 180183,
    "observedFrom": "2026-10-26T05:10:38.805852+00:00",
    "isBounded": true,
    "coverage": "Observed changes only: what a refresh of this source detected, from when change capture began for it through today, retained 5 years. ..."
  }
}
```

`isBounded: true` here because 2026-06-01 predates `observedFrom`. The text is our best reconstruction,
and a product built on it should say so to its reader.

A U.S. Code date that no held edition governs answers like this instead, with no text and no charge:

```json
{
  "actId": "USC_T29_C8_S207",
  "plain": null,
  "available": false,
  "asOf": {
    "requested": "2019-06-01",
    "engine": "stored_edition",
    "source": "unavailable",
    "existed": false,
    "isBounded": false,
    "outOfCoverageReason": "We hold no published edition governing 2019-06-01, so the text in force on that date is not served. The earliest edition we hold is dated 2025-01-06, which is after your date."
  }
}
```

## Choosing the right tool

| Question | Use |
|---|---|
| What did this section say on a date? | `asOf` on `/section/{actId}/body` |
| What changed, and when? | [`/section/{actId}/changes`](/api-guide/concepts/section-intelligence) |
| Give me the whole 2004 CFR as printed | `corpusType=CFR_ANNUAL` |
| Tell me when it changes from now on | [Law change alerts](/api-guide/alerts) |
| Which sections moved since my last sync? | `changedSince` on `/statutes/search` |
