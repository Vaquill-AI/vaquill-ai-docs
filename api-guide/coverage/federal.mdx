## Scope limits by corpus

Read the entry for any corpus you are about to rely on. Each one states what is deliberately
not held.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="United States Code - an annual publication, so weekly means a weekly check">
  The source is the federal publisher's own annual `USCODE` package on GovInfo, issued once per
  edition rather than amended in place. A weekly job asks GovInfo whether a newer edition exists
  and re-ingests the whole Code when one appears. Between editions the text does not move.

  The edition served is **2024, current through 2025-01-06**. For federal law enacted after that
  date use `corpusType=SESSION_LAW`, which does refresh weekly, or `corpusType=FEDERAL_REGISTER`
  for agency rulemaking.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Statutes at Large - as enacted, so deliberately not current law">
  Federal public and private laws **as enacted**, session by session, 1789-2026, from 137 of the
  140 volumes. Volumes 7, 8 and 44 are not held. This is the source the US Code is compiled from,
  so a US Code section's `publicLawCites` resolve here to the enacting text.

  Sections resolve by `actId`, for example `SAL_PL116-136_DVA_TII_S1109` (CARES Act section 1109).

  Because these are laws as enacted they are a historical record rather than a statement of
  current law: they carry `actStatus: "enacted"` and `goodLawStatus: "unknown"` deliberately.
  For what the law says today, use `corpusType=USC`.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="CFR annual editions - a section observed at published dates, not extra breadth">
  GPO's officially published annual edition of the CFR, 31 editions from 1996 to 2026, all 49
  titles. A section is stored once per distinct run of text rather than once per edition, so
  312,132 distinct sections are held as 886,066 section versions and served as 2,844,809 passages.

  The section count is what enters the federal total. This is the same body of regulation as the
  `CFR` corpus observed at published dates, not additional breadth.

  Two behaviors a caller needs. **`editionsObserved` is an enumeration, never a range**: a version
  observed in 1998, 1999 and 2003 lists exactly those years, and a date in an unobserved gap comes
  back with `asOf.isBounded: false` rather than the nearest year held. **A citation year is the
  volume's own `<REVISED>` year, not the folder GPO filed it under**, because GPO reprints
  unchanged volumes forward without re-dating them; 167 versions in title 26 sit in the 1997
  folder stamped 1990.

  `CFR_ANNUAL` serves an edition as published, while `asOf` on
  `GET /us/statutes/section/{actId}/body` reconstructs a section from observed change history.
  See [Point-in-time law](/api-guide/point-in-time) for when each applies.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Federal Register notices - a curated 2.3% slice, not the notice series">
  **Held:** notices that act on a rulemaking, which is withdrawing a proposed rule, extending or
  reopening a comment period, publishing significant agency guidance, and announcing negotiated
  rulemaking. That is 17,612 of the 766,073 notices published in the period, about 2.3%. Every
  point carries `frNoticeClass` naming the rule that admitted it.

  **Not held:** Paperwork Reduction Act information collections, meeting notices, permits and
  applications, antidumping proceedings, Privacy Act system-of-records notices, and the
  unclassified remainder. Do not read this token as complete notice coverage.

  Notices carry an **empty CFR cross-reference graph** (`crossReferencesCfr` and `frTopics` at 0%,
  `frRegulationIdNumbers` at 2%, against 100% and 94% on rules), so a notice is not reachable by a
  CFR-citation filter. `frDocketIds` at 73.5% is the join key that works. A notice amends nothing,
  so `amendmentYears` is empty and `goodLawStatus` is `unknown`.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Administrative adjudications - seven bodies, and no overruling signal">
  Federal matters decided by an agency through a formal process, with precedential weight on that
  body's later decisions.

  | Body | Sections | `source` |
  |---|---:|---|
  | FTC Part 3 administrative filings, 1995 to date | 18,023 | `ftc_administrative_decision` |
  | SEC opinions and adjudicatory orders, 1996 to date | 5,790 | `sec_commission_opinion` |
  | Merit Systems Protection Board | 5,461 | `mspb_precedential`, `mspb_nonprecedential` |
  | BIA and Attorney General precedent decisions | 3,292 | `bia_precedent`, `ag_precedent`, `immigration_admin_precedent` |
  | NLRB decisions, 2012 to date | 2,762 | `nlrb_board_decision` |
  | DOJ Office of Legal Counsel | 1,371 | `olc_opinion` |
  | CFPB enforcement actions | 386 | `cfpb_enforcement_action` |

  Citation forms: `FTC Docket 9436`, `Exchange Act Release No. 34-106254`, `YYYY MSPB N`,
  `I&N Dec.`, `NNN NLRB No. N`, `NN Op. O.L.C. NNN`, Bureau File No. `2024-CFPB-0002`.

  **`goodLawStatus` is `unknown` on every body except the CFPB slice.** No publisher here emits a
  machine-readable overruling signal, so a decision is served as issued and whether a later
  decision overruled it is not asserted.

  Five further scope limits:

  - The FTC slice is the **Part 3 administrative body only**, 1,431 records carrying the
    Commission's own `Administrative` action type. Its 2,039 federal-court records and roughly
    2,636 records carrying neither action type are not held. FTC rules are excluded because they
    are codified at 16 C.F.R. and served under `corpusType=CFR`.
  - **The FTC unit is a FILING, not a decision.** A Part 3 docket is mostly documents written by
    the parties: 21% of passages are the operative instruments (complaints, decisions and orders,
    consent agreements) and the rest are motions, notices of appearance, exhibits and
    public-comment analyses. Nothing is dropped on that basis, the whole docket is served, but the
    per-filing label is not yet a response field or a search filter.
  - The NLRB slice is a **bounded 15-year window, 2012 to date**, of a 66,612-decision archive.
  - **OLC opinions issued in 2021 and later are not included.** They have not appeared in a bound
    volume, and the Department's slip-opinion listing cannot be paginated.
  - **SEC ALJ initial decisions are not held**, and neither are BIA reporter volumes 1 to 7,
    which EOIR does not publish.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="US tax treaties - about 60% of the Senate record has no instrument text">
  Two sources under one `corpusType=US_TAX_TREATY`, 19,646 retrieval passages.

  **The IRS mirror** (173, `us_tax_treaty` and `us_tax_treaty_technical_explanation`): 71
  bilateral income tax conventions for 68 countries, 71 Treasury Technical Explanations, 23
  protocols, 6 exchanges of notes, 1 memorandum of understanding, 1 negotiation statement. A
  protocol is served as its own document rather than folded into the convention it amends, because
  a protocol is what moves withholding rates, limitation-on-benefits articles and entry-into-force
  dates. No estate or gift tax treaties.

  **The Senate record** (1,476): all 780 treaties the Senate has considered since 1949, tax and
  non-tax alike. `senate_treaty` (780) carries title, parties, subject index and dated procedural
  history; `senate_treaty_resolution` (406) is the resolution of advice and consent, where the
  reservations, understandings and declarations that narrow a treaty's effect in US law live;
  `senate_treaty_document` (290) is the President's transmittal message, from GPO.

  Two limits. **About 60% of the Senate record is metadata with no instrument text** from either
  publisher, which `source` states rather than hides. **Termination is not tracked**, because no
  source held states it, so `goodLawStatus` is `unknown` on every record.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Statute Compilations - an act as amended through a stated public law">
  An act's own text **as amended through** a stated later public law, from GovInfo's official
  compilations. Act-organized rather than codified, so a citation like "Section 210 of the Public
  Utility Regulatory Policies Act" resolves here even though that section was never codified into
  a positive-law title and has no `USC` address.

  Distinct from Statutes at Large, which holds the same acts as enacted. Each section states its
  own currency in `currentThrough`, ranging from `Public Law 96-308` (1980) to `Public Law 119-75`
  (2026). **2.6% of compilations state no currency at all** and carry
  `compilationCurrencyState: "unstated"` rather than a guessed date. Cross-references into `USC`
  are captured as `crossReferencesUsc`.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="US Sentencing Guidelines - former versions are separate documents">
  The 302 current guideline sections, plus Appendix A (the statutory index) and Appendix B, whose
  Part III carries the text of FORMER guidelines and commentary as they read before amendment.
  The former versions are separate documents, not variants of the current section.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
