Sub-regulatory federal guidance: what an agency says its own rules mean, short of a regulation.
**53 named sources across 26 agencies.**
All sit behind the single `corpusType=AGENCY_GUIDANCE` token.
Narrow to one with `source`.
A new source lands as a registry entry, not a new API token.

```json
{ "query": "tip credit", "corpusType": "AGENCY_GUIDANCE", "source": "whd_foh" }
```

State insurance and financial bulletins are a different corpus: see the [state coverage matrix](/api-guide/coverage/states).

<Note>
  Counts here cover the 53 named sources (87,082 documents).
  The token holds more: guidance not attributed to a named source carries the token without a `source` you can filter on, and the token's own total is in the [federal coverage table](/api-guide/coverage/federal).
  The live total and the list of sources come from `GET /us/statutes/coverage`, which wins where this page differs.
  An invalid `source` returns the full list with each code's name.
</Note>

<Accordion title="Why totals differ">
  `GET /us/statutes/coverage` computes its `AGENCY_GUIDANCE` total out of band rather than per request, so for up to a day after a source is ingested it can lag the corpus.
  The column above sums to 87,082, measured per source on 2026-09-07, plus the two FTC sources added on 2026-09-08 and measured that day.
  On 2026-10-13 the endpoint reported 96,966 for the whole token, 9,884 more than the column, and on 2026-11-07 it reported 97,114.
  This page does not break that difference down by source.
  Every row is a served count, not an ingested one.
  Ingested and served counts differ most on IRS written determinations, whose figures are reconciled once in the `irs_written_determination` note below.
  The endpoint stays authoritative.
</Accordion>

## goodLawStatus by source

Where a publisher states no retirement record for its guidance, `goodLawStatus` is `unknown`.
`unknown` is a deliberate answer, not a gap, and it is never a soft yes.
The rule is explained in [status and currency](/api-guide/concepts/status-and-currency).
Where a publisher does state a status, it is read from the source rather than defaulted.

| `source` | `goodLawStatus` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `irs_irm` | `unknown` | No IRS source states when an IRM section is superseded. |
| `cms_iom` | `unknown` | Manual provisions are not binding rules, and every passage says so in `currencyNote`. |
| `whd_foh` | `unknown` | WHD says its pages may not reflect current legislation, regulations or court decisions. |
| `ftc_advisory_opinion` | `unknown` | The Commission publishes no machine-readable withdrawal signal. |
| `ftc_policy_statement` | `unknown` | Same reason as the advisory opinions. |
| `ftc_hsr_interpretation` | `unknown` | Supersession relations are served as `supersedes` and `supersededBy`, and no document is marked dead on the strength of them. |
| `ftc_advocacy_filing` | `unknown` | Advisory only. |
| `doj_business_review` | `unknown` | The Division publishes no machine-readable withdrawal signal, and every passage says so. |
| `irs_written_determination` | Read from source | Every document is `actStatus: "non_precedential"` by statute. |
| `whd_opinion_letter` | Read from source | `actStatus` comes from DOL's own footnote table and disclaimer lines. |
| `eeoc_guidance` | Read from source | Read from the retirement banner EEOC prints on the document. |
| `ofac_faq` | Read from source | Prior versions are `superseded`. |
| `fincen_boi_rule_qa` | Read from source | The 10 interim-rule Q&As are `superseded`. |

## Source notes

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="irs_written_determination - by statute, not citable as precedent">
  IRS Written Determinations under 26 U.S.C. § 6110: **28,641 Private Letter Rulings, 5,573 Chief Counsel Advice memoranda, 5,482 Tax Exempt and Government Entities determination letters and 842 Technical Advice Memoranda**, plus 4,820 the IRS does not type on the document itself.
  It is the largest source in this token by document count.

  Each is directed only to the taxpayer who requested it, and § 6110(k)(3) says it "may not be used or cited as precedent".
  The caveat comes from the statute, and it is why every one of the 45,358 ingested carries `actStatus: "non_precedential"` rather than `in_force`.
  Read one as evidence of how the Service analysed one taxpayer's facts, never as a rule of general application.

  Coverage runs from 1999-01-08 to 2026-09-04, 28 distinct years.
  The figures reconcile like this:

  | Figure | Determinations |
  |---|---:|
  | Listed by the IRS | 45,431 |
  | Ingested | 45,358 |
  | Served through the API | 35,485 (78.1% of listed) |
  | Ingested, not currently reachable (OCR-read scans) | 9,873 |
  | Listed, not ingested | 73 |

  The 9,873 scans were read by OCR rather than from a text layer, because the IRS publishes them as scans of signed paper.
  Two consequences matter before you rely on their text.
  The section symbol survives OCR less reliably than the words around it, so a passage may read `section 501(c)(3)` where the page prints `§ 501(c)(3)`; search for either.
  A stamped control number in a letterhead can also lose a digit, which is why the document's own `actId` and `citation` are taken from the IRS index rather than from the scanned text.

  The 73 not ingested: 56 are scans no OCR could read, 14 failed a legibility check, and 3 are not PDFs at the publisher's end.
  Each is counted, so none is a silent gap.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="irs_irm - agency procedure, not law">
  The IRS's own instruction manual for its employees.
  Every passage is labeled "IRS internal procedure".
  Do not read an IRM response timeframe as a statutory deadline.

  Each section carries the Effective Date the IRS prints on it.
  54 sections (3.0%) carry no Manual Transmittal block and take their amendment year from per-subsection revision stamps.
  IRM 2.4.19 is indexed by the IRS but served as an empty page, and is not ingested.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="cms_iom - agency procedure, and five publications are near-empty">
  How Medicare runs day to day: Pub. 100-04 says what a provider may bill, 100-02 what Medicare covers, 100-08 what a contractor reads before an audit.

  Not law.
  Courts have repeatedly held that manual provisions are not binding substantive rules; the binding requirements are in the Social Security Act and 42 C.F.R. Every passage says so in `currencyNote`.

  98.4% of sections carry CMS's own `(Rev. N, Issued, Effective, Implementation)` stamp, spanning 2000 to 2026 across 27 distinct years.

  Five of CMS's 25 publications hold nothing or almost nothing, and those gaps are the publisher's: 100-12, 100-13 and 100-23 publish no chapters at all, 100-21 is titled Reserved, and 100-18 (Part D) is a two-page table of contents whose own text says several chapters were never disseminated outside HPMS.
  Chapters are replaced in place and CMS publishes no superseded editions, so `asOf` is not bounded for this corpus.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="whd_foh - enforcement procedure, with unusually good versioning">
  How the Wage and Hour Division instructs its own investigators to read the FLSA, FMLA, SCA, DBRA, CCPA, MSPA and section 14(c), at the operational detail the regulations do not reach.
  It is the companion to `whd_opinion_letter`, which it cites and does not contain.

  Not law, and WHD says so: its own page states these pages "may not reflect current legislation, regulations, significant court decisions" and that the Federal Register and the CFR "remain the official resources".

  Revision history comes in three forms.
  All 22 chapters carry WHD's `Source: FOH revision N, published DATE` stamp, 90 provisions (6.0%) carry a provision-level revision date, and 1,267 (84.5%) carry at least one dated row from WHD's pre-2016 revision tables.

  Chapter numbering is not contiguous (10-16, 19-25, 30-33, 36, 39, 46, 64).
  The missing numbers return 404 from WHD itself.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="whd_opinion_letter - a real, machine-readable withdrawal signal">
  WHD's written answers to a named requester's fact-specific question: FLSA (417 letters), FMLA (154), then SCA, DBRA, CCPA, MSPA, child labor and agriculture.
  Four publisher series: 363 Opinion letters, 211 Non-Opinion letters, 13 DBRA and SCA ruling letters, and the 7 Administrator Interpretations that replaced opinion letters entirely between 2010 and 2017 and are the only documents covering that gap.

  WHD publishes a machine-readable withdrawal signal, which is unusual for sub-regulatory guidance, so `actStatus` is read off DOL's own footnote table and disclaimer lines rather than inferred: 561 `in_force`, 28 `withdrawn`, 3 `superseded`, 2 `vacated` by the Administrative Review Board.
  Most withdrawals are the March 2, 2009 mass withdrawal, 17 of which WHD re-issued in 2018 under new numbers.

  A re-issued pair shares one PDF on dol.gov but is two documents with two numbers, two dates and opposite status, so each is served under its own `act_id` and the predecessor stays retrievable as withdrawn.

  The publisher's index lists 596 records and 594 are served.
  The two missing are a publisher defect, detailed under "Publisher-side gaps" below.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="eeoc_guidance - the retirement banner is the only signal EEOC publishes">
  The Commission's own interpretation of the statutes it enforces, including Compliance Manual sections.
  By authority cited: Title VII (98 documents), the ADA (92), the Rehabilitation Act (70), the ADEA (45), GINA (32), the Equal Pay Act (27).

  `act_id` is the URL slug rather than EEOC's OLC control number, which is carried as metadata (178 documents print one, and they hold only 177 distinct values).

  `goodLawStatus` is read from EEOC's own banner rather than defaulted: 172 documents are `in_force`, 6 `superseded`, 1 `rescinded`, each dead document carrying the Commission's own sentence as evidence.
  That banner is the only retirement record EEOC publishes, so this is a verdict about retirements EEOC announced on the document itself; guidance the Commission quietly stopped relying on reads `in_force`.

  Federal-sector appellate decisions are not here.
  They are decided cases and belong under `corpusType=AGENCY_ADJUDICATION`, and they are reachable only through a search path the agency restricts automated access to.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ftc_advisory_opinion - 206 of 430 are staff letters indexed under several sections">
  The Commission's and its staff's answers to conduct a requester proposes, under 16 C.F.R.
  §§ 1.1-1.4, across the Franchise Rule, the Holder Rule, the Funeral Rule, the FCRA and health-care antitrust.
  An advisory opinion binds only its requester and decides no case, which is why it sits here and not under `AGENCY_ADJUDICATION`.

  206 of the 430 have no entry of their own in the FTC's listing.
  The Commission's FCRA topic pages index the staff letters answering each section of the Act, one letter under as many sections as it answers, and each is served as its own citable document.

  11 documents carry no date the FTC prints.
  Four documents carry publisher-side defects, served as the Commission serves them and detailed under "Publisher-side gaps" below.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ftc_hsr_interpretation - one series, two halves, and 1997 is missing at the source">
  The Hart-Scott-Rodino premerger notification interpretations, both halves under one `source`.
  A **formal** interpretation is the Commission's own authoritative construction of a Premerger Notification Rule under 16 C.F.R. 803.30: a closed series of 20, issued 1978 to 2000, with none since.
  An **informal** interpretation is the Premerger Notification Office answering a practitioner's question about the same rules: 4,569 of them, 1983 to 2025.
  Filter the two apart with `documentSubtype`; join them on `construedRule`, which carries the rule number the interpretation is about on 99.0% of documents.

  1997 is entirely absent at the publisher.
  The FTC serves no informal interpretation numbered for that year at all, against 141 in 1996 and 83 in 1998, so roughly 100 to 140 interpretations from 1997 are missing from the source rather than from this corpus.

  The FTC's own listing counts 4,759, which is a link count: 186 interpretations are served twice under a duplicate page.
  Each is held once, under the interpretation number the Commission assigns.

  An informal interpretation reflects staff views, not Commission action, and is expressly not binding on the Commission.
  Where the record also attaches an "Original Image" scan of the same letter, it is listed in `relatedDocuments` and not mirrored: it is a redacted photocopy of text the document already carries in full.

  The Commission states a supersession in the slug of one interpretation and publishes three dated versions of another; those relations are served as `supersedes` / `supersededBy`, and no document is marked dead on the strength of them.
  `effectiveDate` is absent on 13 of the 20 formal interpretations, because the FTC publishes no date field for that series and those 13 print dates only inside their own argument, where the document's own date cannot be told apart from the dates it cites.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ftc_advocacy_filing - advisory only: these bind nobody">
  Comments and letters the Commission and its staff address to state legislatures, state supreme courts, governors and other federal agencies about the competition effects of a proposed measure.
  860 filings, 1985 to 2026.

  They bind nobody.
  Unlike every other source under `AGENCY_GUIDANCE`, an advocacy filing is not the agency telling the regulated public how it reads a law it administers: it decides no case, construes no FTC rule, and the recipient is free to ignore it.
  It is held as its own `source` with its own `forum` (`ftc_advocacy`) so it can be scoped out in one parameter, and every document says so in its own `coverageNote`.

  The Matter Number is a program-level number the FTC reuses across many filings (one covers 29 different documents).
  It identifies a program, not a document, and is served as `caseNumber` rather than as a citation.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ftc_policy_statement - what Section 5 means, as opposed to one requester's answer">
  The Commission's own statements of what Section 5 means: the Policy Statement on Deception (1983), on Unfairness (1980), the 2022 statement on the scope of unfair methods of competition, and the FTC-DOJ antitrust and merger guidelines.
  `effective_date` is on 100%.
  This is the standard the Commission's Part 3 orders apply.

  The Commission's 868-page Compendium is excluded because it re-publishes material already served under other `act_id`s. 6 further records exist as a citation only, with a title and a date and no document, and are recorded as out of scope rather than served as empty.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="copyright_compendium - point-in-time is the publisher's own answer">
  The administrative manual the Copyright Office applies to every registration and recordation, section-addressable at its own `section NNN.N` numbering across 24 chapters.
  2,455 current sections; 5,920 across three editions (2014-12-22, 2017-09-29, 2021-01-28).

  All three dated releases are stored separately and `asOf` resolves against the edition the Office states was in force, so point-in-time here is the publisher's answer rather than a reconstruction.
  Amendment history covers the 2021 release only (719 amended, 101 renumbered sections); the Office publishes no change log for 2017 or 2014.

  An `asOf` answer is the manual in force on that date, not the law.
  The Office's own exception for "an amendment to the regulations, intervening case law, or previously announced changes in practice" is served verbatim on every section.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="doj_business_review - no withdrawal signal, and one broken publisher link">
  The Antitrust Division's statements of its enforcement intentions on proposed conduct under 28 C.F.R. § 50.6, each served as the response letter with the requesting party's own request letter appended.

  215 of the 216 letters listed are served; the 216th (97-16) is a broken link on the Division's own site.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ofac_faq - prior versions are separate, citable documents">
  OFAC's own interpretation of the sanctions regulations it administers at 31 C.F.R. chapter V, across all 38 sanctions programs.
  989 current FAQs plus 361 prior versions.

  Prior versions come from OFAC's own FAQ Archive and are served as separate, citable documents with `actStatus: "superseded"`, so `asOf` on a FAQ returns the text OFAC published on that date rather than a reconstruction.
  238 FAQs (24.1%) carry at least one observed revision; the deepest is FAQ 999 with 12 versions.

  The revision trail starts 2023-03-22 and goes no further back.
  OFAC migrated its FAQs to a new website that day and stamped every then-active FAQ with the migration date rather than its original publication date, so 860 version records carry no `effectiveDate`.

  OFAC has rescinded 279 FAQ numbers and publishes nothing for any of them, so a rescinded FAQ is absent rather than marked withdrawn.
  Sanctions lists (SDN, consolidated) and General Licenses are not included; General License links are served as cross-references.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ferc_policy_statement - narrower than eLibrary">
  FERC's generally applicable interpretive vehicle.
  The source is narrower than the docket-based eLibrary universe of case-specific orders, which is not held.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="doe_appliance_guidance and hhs_ocr_hipaa_faq - range caveats">
  `doe_appliance_guidance`: draft guidance is excluded.

  `hhs_ocr_hipaa_faq`: deduplicated across topic pages.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="administrative_guidance - reference figures, assembled and not agency text">
  Annually adjusted dollar thresholds that do not live in USC or CFR section text: the state and local tax (SALT) cap, the Child Tax Credit, 401(k) limits, the Subchapter V debt limit.
  Each is primary-sourced but assembled by us rather than published as a single agency document, so it is named separately from the agency sources above.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="fincen_boi_* - the August 2026 final rule, and what to disregard">
  On August 11, 2026 FinCEN issued a final rule that permanently narrowed BOI reporting.
  Entities created in the United States and their beneficial owners are now exempt; only certain companies formed under foreign law and registered to do business in a US State or tribal jurisdiction must report, and they do not report BOI for US-person beneficial owners or company applicants.

  `fincen_boi_faq` (issued 2023-2024) and `fincen_boi_compliance_guide` (v1.4, March 2025) both predate that rule.
  We serve them as published, because they remain the authoritative FinCEN text and much of it, such as the beneficial-owner and substantial-control definitions in FAQ category D, is untouched by the scope change.
  Every passage carries FinCEN's own notice verbatim in `currencyNote`, with `currencyYear: 2026`.

  Disregard any statement in them that US companies or their beneficial owners must report BOI, that BOI must be reported for US persons, that US persons must update or correct FinCEN ID information, or that reports were due before April 25, 2025.

  **For the current position use `fincen_boi_rule_qa`.**
  Its 12 final-rule Q&As are current; its 10 interim-rule Q&As carry `actStatus: "superseded"` and are excluded from good-law retrieval.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Publisher-side gaps">
  These are defects at the publisher, not in the corpus.
  Each document is served as the publisher serves it.

  - `whd_opinion_letter`: FLSA2009-1NA and FLSA2009-2NA carry a truncated PDF path with no filename that 404s on dol.gov itself, so they are recorded as fetch failures.
    The numbering is intact otherwise: every FLSA year runs 1 to max with no gap, and FMLA-1 to FMLA-113 is complete.
  - `whd_foh`: chapter numbering is not contiguous (10-16, 19-25, 30-33, 36, 39, 46, 64), and the missing numbers return 404 from WHD itself.
  - `eeoc_guidance`: the enumeration is EEOC's own sitemap filtered to `/laws/guidance/`, which yields all 179 documents.
    EEOC's `/eeoc-guidance` hub renders only 56 of them with no pager, so building from the hub would cover about a third of the corpus.
    `EEOC-NVTA-2016-3` is printed on two unrelated documents.
    `/rescinded-guidance`, `/withdrawn-guidance` and `/laws/guidance/rescinded` all 404.
    The word "Rescinded" on EEOC's hub is a filing instruction about a neighboring manual section ("Appears after Rescinded 604"), not a status.
  - `ftc_advisory_opinion`: `advisory-opinion-coffey-02-11-98` is truncated mid-word on ftc.gov, and `informal-staff-advisory-opinion-97-2` and `-97-3` are byte-identical there, so one of those pages carries the wrong letter.
</Accordion>
