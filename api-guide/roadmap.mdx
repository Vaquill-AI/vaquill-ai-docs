This page lists only what is not built yet.
It is direction only, and no date on it is a commitment.

For what is queryable right now, `GET /us/statutes/coverage` is the live answer and this page is not.
It reports every jurisdiction, every corpus and every section count at the moment you call it, it is free, and it needs no authentication beyond your key.
Poll it to detect new corpora.

Everything below would ship the way the current data does: from an official government source, citation-linked and refreshed on a schedule.
Each corpus lands behind the same `corpusType` filter, so there is nothing to integrate.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Point-in-time and as-applied federal law">
    The current text of a rule answers what it says today.
    It does not answer what it said on the date of the conduct, which is the question most compliance disputes actually turn on.

    The **LSA** is the official month-by-month map from a CFR section to the change that hit it and the Federal Register page that made it.
    It is the join key between the `CFR` and `FEDERAL_REGISTER` corpora already in the API, and it answers "what changed this month" directly.
    With the annual editions live, the LSA is what would explain why a section differs between two adjacent editions.

    **FAR and DFARS class deviations** matter because, since the Federal Acquisition Regulation overhaul, agencies have been applying model deviation text that 48 C.F.R. does not yet reflect.
    FAR and DFARS are already served inside `CFR`; the deviations are what a contracting officer is operating under in the meantime.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Federal local rules">
    Federal local rules are adopted under 28 U.S.C. 2071 and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 83.
    They modify the national Federal Rules in every district, so the national rules alone are an incomplete picture of what governs a filing.
    They are citable in their own form, for example `N.D. Cal. Civ. L.R. 7-2`.

    This is a large item: each remaining court publishes its own set, mostly as PDFs, with no common schema.
    What is already served is in [Federal coverage](/api-guide/coverage/federal).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="DOL adjudication bodies">
    The DOL bodies span an 80+ case-type taxonomy: SOX, AIR21, STAA, ERA, OSH, OFCCP, LCA, black lung and longshore, among others.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Pending federal legislation">
    Congressional bills are not held today, and no date is set for them.
    The `FEDERAL_BILL` token holds proposed text only, labelled proposed, never law and never part of an unscoped search.
    The token is reserved: it is registered but returns no results today.
    The enacted text of a bill that became law is served under `corpusType=SESSION_LAW` (Statutes at Large) and, once codified, under `corpusType=USC`.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Out of scope

These are deliberate exclusions.

| Not building | Why |
|---|---|
| General case law and court dockets | The corpus is primary law: statutes, regulations, constitutions, court rules, agency material. |
| GAO and Comptroller General decisions | GAO does not permit automated access to its decisions and offers no feed or API. |
| Judge-specific procedural material (standing orders, chambers procedures, individual practice rules) | It has no stable location, citation form or amendment cycle. |
| Practice notes and practice directions | A Commonwealth instrument with no direct US federal equivalent. The closest analogues are local rules and court-wide general orders. |
| Municipal and county ordinances | Public-domain text, but locked in three commercial publisher platforms with no comprehensive source. |
| Standards incorporated by reference into the CFR (ASTM, NFPA, ISO) | Published by private standards bodies. Courts have held only non-commercial dissemination is fair use, which is incompatible with a paid API. |
| NAIC model law text | A copyrighted, commercially sold publication, not open government primary law. Each state's own insurance bulletins are in scope under `STATE_AGENCY_GUIDANCE`. |

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="GAO decisions">
    GAO decisions of the Comptroller General on appropriations law and bid protests are a deliberate no.

    GAO does not permit automated access to its decisions, and it offers no feed or API either.
    The only bulk source is govinfo's `GAOREPORTS` collection, which runs from 1989 to 2008 and then stops.
    A bid-protest corpus that ends 18 years ago invites a customer to read absence as a finding, which is worse than not serving it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The line on court material">
    The corpus carries rules a court formally adopts and publishes as binding on everyone appearing before it, not an individual judge's preferences.

    Formally adopted rules are stable, citable, published under statutory authority, and amended on a visible cycle.
    That covers the Federal Rules and Supreme Court Rules today, the state court rules in the [Coverage](/api-guide/coverage) matrix, and the federal local rules.

    Judge-specific material fails every one of those tests: over 1,500 judges, no consistent location, no citation form, revised or voided silently on rotation.
    It could be collected but not kept current, and a confidently stale answer is worse than none.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
