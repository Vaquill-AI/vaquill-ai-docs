This page lists what is coming to the corpus and what is not.
Everything here goes through the same pipeline as the data already in the API: ingested from the official government source, chunked and embedded for hybrid search, citation-linked, and put on a recurring refresh.
So each item arrives queryable through the same `corpusType` filter, with no new integration work on your side.

For what is queryable **today**, see [Coverage](/api-guide/coverage).
For what has already shipped, see the [Changelog](/api-guide/changelog).

<Note>
  Dates are targets, not commitments.
  `GET /statutes/coverage` is self-describing: new corpora appear there as they land, so polling it is the reliable way to detect one rather than tracking this page.
  See [Coverage monitoring](/api-guide/recipes/coverage-monitoring) for a scheduled diff you can wire up.
</Note>

## Completing existing coverage

Two corpus types are live but not yet across all 52 jurisdictions.
Both are rolling out state by state on the same pipeline and the same refresh cadence as the states already covered.

| Corpus | Today | Target |
|---|---|---|
| State regulations (`REGULATION`) | 45 states | All 52 jurisdictions |
| State court rules (`STATE_RULES`) | 10 states | Broad state coverage, prioritized by demand |

Pennsylvania's Consolidated Statutes are complete; its older unconsolidated (Purdon's) statutes are a separate later addition, prioritized for backfill.

## Federal law and court rules

Targeted to land by the **start of Q4 2026**.

| Source | What it adds | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Register notices | The third Federal Register document class. We carry final and proposed rules today; notices cover meeting announcements, information collections, agency determinations, and grant availability. | govinfo, federalregister.gov |
| Public Laws / Statutes at Large | Slip laws as enacted, before codification into the US Code. Public law numbers appear as a metadata field today, but the acts themselves are not served as documents. | govinfo |
| OLC and Attorney General opinions | Office of Legal Counsel opinions plus formal AG opinions, the executive branch's own binding legal interpretations. | justice.gov/olc |
| GAO decisions | Comptroller General decisions on appropriations law and bid protests. | gao.gov |

## Administrative adjudication

A new corpus type: the record of what federal agencies actually decided, as opposed to what the rules say.
These are public domain under 17 U.S.C. 105 and are genuinely under-aggregated, since each body publishes into its own silo with no shared format or API.

| Body | What it adds |
|---|---|
| SEC administrative proceedings | The full order stream, including AAERs and Rule 102(e) accountant disqualifications. Roughly 18,400 proceedings since the early 2000s. |
| DOL OALJ, ARB and BRB | Administrative law judge decisions plus the Administrative Review Board and Benefits Review Board, across an 80+ case-type taxonomy (SOX, AIR21, STAA, ERA, OSH, OFCCP, LCA, black lung, longshore). |
| NLRB ALJ and Board decisions | Extends the NLRB General Counsel and Division of Advice memoranda already in the corpus. |
| MSPB | Precedential and nonprecedential decisions. |
| FDIC, OCC and Federal Reserve | Enforcement actions and consent orders. Extends the guidance from these agencies already in the corpus. |

On customer demand: OSHRC, FMSHRC, FERC, CFTC reparations, BIA, Tax Court, ASBCA/CBCA, EPA Environmental Appeals Board, FTC administrative decisions, and HHS DAB.

## Out of scope

Being explicit about what we will not build is as useful as listing what we will.
If you need any of the below, we are not the right supplier and would rather tell you now.

**General case law and court dockets.**
Deliberately out of scope, not a gap we are closing.
Vaquill AI is a primary-law corpus: statutes, regulations, constitutions, court rules, and agency material.

**Judge-specific and court-specific procedural material.**
Standing orders, individual judges' practice rules, chambers procedures, and scheduling preferences.
In the US these are issued per judge across 94 federal district courts and every state trial court, with no central publication, so there is no authoritative source to ingest.
Court rules that are formally adopted and published by a court system, including local rules at the state level, are in scope and covered today for the states in the [Coverage](/api-guide/coverage) matrix.

**Municipal and county ordinances.**
The text is public domain, but it sits behind three commercial publisher platforms and there is no comprehensive source.

**Standards incorporated by reference into the CFR.**
Material such as ASTM, NFPA and ISO standards cited by regulation but published by private standards bodies.
Courts have held that only non-commercial dissemination of these is fair use, which makes them incompatible with a paid API.

**NAIC model law text.**
A copyrighted, commercially sold publication rather than open government primary law.
Each state's own published insurance bulletins, issued under that state's own authority, are in scope and covered as a pilot.
