What is coming to the corpus, and what is not.

Everything here ships through the same pipeline as the current data: official government source, chunked and embedded, citation-linked, on a recurring refresh. Each lands behind the same `corpusType` filter, so there is nothing to integrate.

Queryable today: [Coverage](/api-guide/coverage). Already shipped: [Changelog](/api-guide/changelog).

<Note>
  Dates are targets, not commitments. Poll `GET /statutes/coverage` to detect new corpora rather than tracking this page, and see [Coverage monitoring](/api-guide/recipes/coverage-monitoring) for a scheduled diff.
</Note>

## Completing existing coverage

**State regulations reached all 52 jurisdictions on 2026-08-27.** Two corpus types are live but not yet complete.
Both are rolling out state by state on the same pipeline and the same refresh cadence as the states already covered.

| Corpus | Today | Target |
|---|---|---|
| State regulations (`REGULATION`) | **All 52 jurisdictions. Complete.** | Reached 2026-08-27 |
| State court rules (`STATE_RULES`) | 46 jurisdictions | Broad coverage. The remaining states either publish no consolidated rules text from a government source, codify their rules into the statute book (already served under `STATE`), or restrict automated access by published court policy. |
| State agency guidance (`STATE_AGENCY_GUIDANCE`) | 49 jurisdictions, 12,808 documents | Effectively complete; three jurisdictions publish no browsable bulletin archive |

State agency guidance is each insurance regulator's own bulletins and circular
letters, the state-level analog of federal `AGENCY_GUIDANCE`. Three
jurisdictions publish no cleanly scopable insurance-only archive and are not
currently planned: Pennsylvania, Colorado and Louisiana.

Pennsylvania's Consolidated Statutes are complete; its older unconsolidated (Purdon's) statutes are a separate later addition, prioritized for backfill.

## Point-in-time and as-applied federal law

The current text of a rule answers what it says today.
It does not answer what it said on the date of the conduct, which is the question most compliance disputes actually turn on.
These are the highest-priority items on this page.

| Source | What it adds | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| CFR annual editions, 1996 to 2026 | Thirty-one point-in-time editions of the Code of Federal Regulations, so a section can be retrieved as it read in a given year rather than only as it reads today. The annual edition is also the officially published CFR; what any continuously refreshed corpus serves between editions, ours included, is a current compilation of it. | govinfo |
| List of Sections Affected (LSA), monthly, 1997 to 2026 | The official month-by-month map from a CFR section to the change that hit it and the Federal Register page that made it. It is the join key between the `CFR` and `FEDERAL_REGISTER` corpora already in the API, and it answers "what changed this month" directly. | govinfo |
| FAR and DFARS class deviation text | Since the Federal Acquisition Regulation overhaul, agencies have been applying model deviation text that 48 C.F.R. does not yet reflect. FAR and DFARS are already served inside `CFR`; the deviations are what a contracting officer is operating under in the meantime. | acquisition.gov |

## Federal statutory sources outside the US Code

Citations that resolve to nothing today, because the text they point at is not in a code title.

| Source | What it adds | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Statute Compilations, 2,683 documents | Acts as amended that were never codified into a positive-law title. A citation like "Section 210 of the Public Utility Regulatory Policies Act" does not resolve against `USC` today, because that section lives in the act rather than in a code title. | govinfo |
| Statutes at Large, volumes 1 to 112 (1789 to 1998) | Backfills `SESSION_LAW`, which starts at Public Law 106-1 (1999) today. Until it lands, no pre-1999 public law cite resolves, including ERISA and the Tax Reform Act of 1986. | govinfo |
| Senate treaty documents, 785 | Treaties transmitted to the Senate for advice and consent, carrying the full text of the resolution. Distinct from the bilateral income tax treaties already served under `US_TAX_TREATY`, which cover income tax only. | congress.gov |

## Federal law and court rules

Targeted to land by the **start of Q4 2026**, with the exception noted below.

<Note>
  Federal local rules are the largest item in this table by a wide margin,
  since they mean roughly 200 separate courts each publishing their own set,
  mostly as PDFs, with no common schema. Expect it to trail the other three.
</Note>

| Source | What it adds | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Register notices | The third Federal Register document class. We carry final and proposed rules today; notices cover meeting announcements, information collections, agency determinations, and grant availability. | govinfo, federalregister.gov |
| OLC and Attorney General opinions | Office of Legal Counsel (OLC) opinions plus formal Attorney General (AG) opinions, the executive branch's own binding legal interpretations. | justice.gov/olc |
| GAO decisions | Government Accountability Office (GAO) decisions of the Comptroller General on appropriations law and bid protests. | gao.gov |
| Federal local rules | The local rules of the 94 district courts, the 13 circuits, and the bankruptcy courts, adopted under 28 U.S.C. 2071 and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 83. These modify the national Federal Rules in every district, so the national rules alone are an incomplete picture of what governs a filing. Citable in their own form, for example `N.D. Cal. Civ. L.R. 7-2`. | uscourts.gov and each court's own site |

## Tax

Two additional Internal Revenue Service (IRS) corpora, beyond the IRS notices, revenue procedures, revenue rulings and announcements already served under agency guidance.

| Source | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Chief Counsel Advice | Written determinations issued by IRS Chief Counsel to field offices. |
| Private Letter Rulings | Taxpayer-specific rulings on the application of tax law to a proposed transaction. |
| Internal Revenue Manual (IRM), 1,777 sections | The IRS's own operating procedures: what examiners, appeals officers and collection staff are instructed to do. It would be served and labelled as agency procedure, not law. It binds IRS personnel, not taxpayers, and confers no rights on them. |

## Administrative adjudication

A new corpus type: the record of what federal agencies actually decided, as opposed to what the rules say.
These are public domain under 17 U.S.C. 105 and are genuinely under-aggregated, since each body publishes into its own silo with no shared format or API.

| Body | What it adds |
|---|---|
| SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) administrative proceedings | The full order stream, including Accounting and Auditing Enforcement Releases (AAERs) and Rule 102(e) accountant disqualifications. Roughly 18,400 proceedings since the early 2000s. |
| DOL (Department of Labor) OALJ, ARB and BRB | Decisions of the Office of Administrative Law Judges (OALJ), the Administrative Review Board (ARB) and the Benefits Review Board (BRB), across an 80+ case-type taxonomy (SOX, AIR21, STAA, ERA, OSH, OFCCP, LCA, black lung, longshore). |
| NLRB (National Labor Relations Board) | Administrative law judge and Board decisions. Extends the NLRB General Counsel and Division of Advice memoranda already in the corpus. |
| MSPB (Merit Systems Protection Board) | Precedential and nonprecedential decisions. |
| FDIC, OCC and Federal Reserve | Enforcement actions and consent orders from the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and the Federal Reserve. Extends the guidance from these agencies already in the corpus. |

On customer demand: the Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission (OSHRC), the Federal Mine Safety and Health Review Commission (FMSHRC), the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) reparations, the Board of Immigration Appeals (BIA), the US Tax Court, the Armed Services and Civilian Boards of Contract Appeals (ASBCA and CBCA), the EPA Environmental Appeals Board, Federal Trade Commission (FTC) administrative decisions, and the Departmental Appeals Board (DAB) of the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

## Federal regulatory dockets

| Source | What it adds |

| SEC EDGAR comment letters | Securities and Exchange Commission staff comment letters (`UPLOAD`) and filer correspondence (`CORRESP`), from the Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis and Retrieval system (EDGAR). |

## Under evaluation

**United States Reports, for official star pagination.**

The text of Supreme Court opinions is available in many places.
The bound volumes are the only place the official page numbers live, and a pin cite in the form `576 U.S. 644, 681` is what survives into a brief.
This is the one case law item we are weighing, and only for the pagination anchor.
It would not change the position immediately below on general case law and dockets.

## Out of scope

Deliberate exclusions, not gaps. If you need one of these, we are not your supplier.

| Not building | Why |
|---|---|
| General case law and court dockets | We are a primary-law corpus: statutes, regulations, constitutions, court rules, agency material. |
| Judge-specific procedural material (standing orders, chambers procedures, individual practice rules) | See the court-material line below. |
| Practice notes and practice directions | A Commonwealth instrument with no direct US federal equivalent. The closest analogues are local rules and court-wide general orders. |
| Municipal and county ordinances | Public-domain text, but locked in three commercial publisher platforms with no comprehensive source. |
| Standards incorporated by reference into the CFR (ASTM, NFPA, ISO) | Published by private standards bodies. Courts have held only non-commercial dissemination is fair use, which is incompatible with a paid API. |
| NAIC model law text | A copyrighted, commercially sold publication, not open government primary law. Each state's own insurance bulletins are in scope and are now covered across 49 jurisdictions under `STATE_AGENCY_GUIDANCE`. |

### The line on court material

**We carry rules a court formally adopts and publishes as binding on everyone appearing before it. We do not carry an individual judge's preferences.**

Formally adopted rules are stable, citable, published under statutory authority, and amended on a visible cycle. That covers the Federal Rules and Supreme Court Rules today, the state court rules in the [Coverage](/api-guide/coverage) matrix, and the federal local rules above.

Judge-specific material fails every one of those tests: over 1,500 judges, no consistent location, no citation form, revised or voided silently on rotation. We could ingest it. We could not keep it honest, and a confidently stale answer is worse than none.
