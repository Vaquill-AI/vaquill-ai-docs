Every US jurisdiction and corpus type the API has ingested: federal law, state statutes, constitutions, court rules, and regulations, each scoped with the `corpusType` filter.

**4,844,957 distinct sections, indexed as 16,201,932 retrieval passages.** That splits 1,363,580 federal and 3,481,377 state and territory.
Measured on **2026-09-06**; query it live from `GET /statutes/coverage` and `GET /statutes/states`.

The **CFR annual editions** corpus added on 2026-09-05 contributes **312,132 distinct CFR sections** to that total, stored as 886,066 point-in-time section **versions** across 2,844,809 passages.
Versions are counted separately from sections throughout this page: one section observed across 31 editions is one section and many versions.
The section figure is the one that enters the total, and it covers the same regulations as the `CFR` corpus observed at published dates, not additional breadth of distinct regulations.

<Warning>
  **The live endpoint has not picked this measurement up yet.**
  `GET /statutes/coverage` reads its section counts from an overlay that an API worker loads at boot, and the workers have not been restarted since the overlay for this measurement was written, so the endpoint still reports 4,180,527.
  The figures on this page are what it returns once that restart lands. Until then, a smaller number from the endpoint is the stale one, and the API has to be refreshed for this page and the endpoint to agree.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Counts here are **distinct sections**, one per provision or document, matching what `GET /statutes/coverage` returns. Longer documents split into several passages for retrieval, so the index holds more entries than sections, over half of them Federal Register rules at 13-15 passages each.

  That does not change what you get back: a citation lookup still resolves to one section with its full text.
</Note>

## Federal

1,363,580 sections across 14 corpus types, all measured 2026-09-06 on the same definition as the page total.

<Warning>
  **Two of these corpora are counted by `/statutes/coverage` but are not selectable with a `corpusType` filter yet**, verified against the deployed OpenAPI document on 2026-09-06: **`CFR_ANNUAL`** and **`FEDERAL_REGISTER_NOTICE`**.
  The published `corpusType` enum carries 17 tokens and neither of these is among them, so a search filtered to either one is rejected.
  Their rows below are marked, and their sections are inside the 1,363,580 total because that is what the coverage endpoint reports.

<Note>
  **`corpusType=CFR_ANNUAL` holds GPO's published annual editions of the CFR, 1996 to 2026.** It landed on 2026-09-05 and is no longer a roadmap item.

  **The table above carries its SECTION count, 312,132, which is what `/statutes/coverage` publishes.** A section is stored once per distinct run of text, not once per edition: a section whose wording never changed from 1996 to 2026 is ONE record whose `editionsObserved` lists every year it was observed in, not thirty-one records. So those 312,132 distinct CFR sections across 49 titles and 31 published editions are held as 886,066 section versions and served as 2,844,809 retrieval passages. Do not read the version count as breadth: it is the same regulations as `corpusType=CFR`, observed at published dates.

  **`corpusType=CFR_ANNUAL` is not accepted by the deployed search filter yet.** The published `corpusType` enum does not carry the token, verified 2026-09-06, so a search scoped to it is rejected even though `/statutes/coverage` counts it.

  Two things to know before querying it.

  **`editionsObserved` is an enumeration, never a range.** A version observed in 1998, 1999 and 2003 lists exactly those years. It does not imply 2000 to 2002, and a date falling in a gap is reported with `asOf.isBounded: false` rather than answered from the nearest year we happen to hold.

  **The year in a citation is the volume's own revision year, not the folder GPO filed it under.** GPO reprints unchanged volumes into the next year's folder without re-dating them, so only the `<REVISED>` date inside the volume is authoritative. That is why a few records carry revision years before 1996: 167 versions in title 26 (parts 502, 503 and 509) sit in the 1997 folder and are genuinely stamped 1990.

  For regulatory text as it reads today use `corpusType=CFR`. Both routes answer a point-in-time question and they are not interchangeable: `CFR_ANNUAL` serves the official edition as published, while `asOf` on `GET /us/statutes/section/{actId}/body` reconstructs a section from observed change history. The annual edition is the officially published CFR; what any continuously refreshed corpus serves between editions, ours included, is a current compilation of it.
</Note>

<Note>
  **The US Code is an annual publication, so "weekly" here means a weekly check, not a weekly update.**
  Our source is the federal publisher's own annual `USCODE` package on GovInfo, which is issued once per edition rather than amended in place.
  A job runs every Monday, asks GovInfo whether an edition newer than the one we hold has been released, and re-ingests the whole Code when one has.
  Between editions the text does not move, so that job correctly reports no change: every run recorded to date has found no new edition, and we are still serving the 2024 edition, current through 2025-01-06.
  For federal law enacted after that date, use `corpusType=SESSION_LAW` (the Statutes at Large), which does refresh weekly, or `corpusType=FEDERAL_REGISTER` for agency rulemaking.
</Note>

<Warning>
  **Corporate Transparency Act: the BOI sources are not all current, and FinCEN
  says so itself.**

  On August 11, 2026 FinCEN issued a final rule that permanently narrowed BOI
  reporting. Entities created in the United States, previously called "domestic
  reporting companies", and their beneficial owners are now exempt; only certain
  companies formed under foreign law and registered to do business in a US State
  or tribal jurisdiction must report, and they do not report BOI for US-person
  beneficial owners or company applicants. This made permanent the interim final
  rule of March 26, 2025.

  The BOI FAQs (issued 2023-2024) and the Small Entity Compliance Guide (v1.4,
  March 2025) both predate that rule, and FinCEN's own pages carry a notice
  saying some of their content is outdated. We serve them as published, because
  they remain the authoritative FinCEN text and much of it, such as the
  beneficial-owner and substantial-control definitions in FAQ category D, is
  untouched by the scope change. Every passage from these sources carries
  FinCEN's notice verbatim in `currencyNote`, with `currencyYear: 2026`.

  Disregard any statement in them that US companies or their beneficial owners
  must report BOI, that BOI must be reported for US persons, that US persons
  must update or correct FinCEN ID information, or that reports were due before
  April 25, 2025.

  For the current position, use the FinCEN BOI Rulemaking Q&As. The 12 final-rule
  Q&As are current; the 10 interim-rule Q&As are served with
  `actStatus: "superseded"` and are excluded from good-law retrieval.
</Warning>

### Why six jurisdictions show no court rules

Every gap is a source problem rather than a coverage decision, and each has a
different cause. Statutes, regulations and constitutions for these
jurisdictions are unaffected.

**[1] No government-published consolidated text** - Arkansas, Vermont.
Both publish their *amendment stream* (promulgation orders, rule changes,
proposed amendments) on the judiciary's own site, but the consolidated current
text is published only by a commercial licensee. The rule text itself is a
government edict and in the public domain; what is missing is a
government-published compilation.

**[2] Only rule-change orders are published** - Colorado.
The judiciary posts adopted and proposed rule changes, not consolidated text,
and the most recent government compilation is from 2023. Ingesting it would
serve superseded procedure as current law, so it is deliberately not ingested.

**[3] Already served under `corpusType=STATE`** - Oklahoma, South Dakota.
Both codify their rules of court into the statute book (Okla. Stat. tit. 12;
SDCL tits. 15, 19 and 23A). Those rules are already in the corpus, cited the way
the state cites them (`SDCL 15-6-12(b)`). Ingesting them again under
`STATE_RULES` would be duplicate coverage, not new coverage.

**[4] Publisher policy** - Missouri.
Missouri's published court policy does not permit automated access to its
judicial websites, and we honor it. Coverage there needs a license from the
state, not a workaround.

## State agency guidance

State-issued regulatory guidance (bulletins, circular letters) that
interprets state law - the state-scoped analog of Agency guidance above.
Unlike statutes, regulations, constitutions, and court rules, this is not on
a comprehensive 52-jurisdiction rollout: it lands state by state, and some
states may never be built. Queryable via `corpusType=STATE_AGENCY_GUIDANCE`,
paired with `state`.

**13,224 documents across 49 jurisdictions.** Almost all of it is insurance-regulator guidance
(`source=state_insurance_bulletin`). Two states, Arizona and Oregon, publish
their **financial-institutions** regulator's guidance on the same index, and
those documents are served separately as `source=state_financial_bulletin` so
an insurance search is never handed a credit-union bulletin. That second
source covers banking, credit unions, trust companies, escrow agents, consumer
lenders, money transmitters, loan originators and investment advisers. It
exists only for these two states, because most states' banking regulators
publish no numbered series and there is nothing to cite; it is not a claim to
cover state banking law.

`source=state_financial_bulletin` is served `goodLawStatus: "unknown"`: neither
regulator has rescinded anything we hold, so there is no measured signal to
certify against. The insurance source is unaffected and keeps its own

## Refresh cadence

How often each corpus is re-pulled from its official source. The counts above are
current as of the date at the top of this page and grow between updates; call
`/statutes/coverage` or `/statutes/states` for the live count of any
jurisdiction.

| Corpus | Cadence |
|---|---|
| Federal | See the [Federal table](#federal): daily to weekly, and on amendment for the Constitution. |
| State statutes | **24 jurisdictions re-pull every 7 days** because their code can be pulled whole; the rest are on a monthly cycle or carry text as of their ingest date. See [Which statute cadence applies where](#which-statute-cadence-applies-where). |
| State regulations | All 52 jurisdictions carry regulations, on a monthly cycle where the publisher supports one. Treat the cadence as per-state rather than uniform, and call [`GET /boards`](/api-guide/alerts) for the age of any one of them: it is free and carries `lastRetrievedAt`, `cadence` and `retrievalStatus` per source. `/statutes/coverage` reports what each corpus is *current through* in the publisher's own words, which is a different question and is not available for every state. |
| State court rules | Monthly, for the states that carry them. Court rules change less often than statutes, and when they do the change is procedural and effective on a published date rather than immediately. |
| State constitutions | Monthly per jurisdiction. Constitutions change on the order of years via ballot-measure amendments, so this is deliberately lower-frequency than statutes and regulations. |

### Which statute cadence applies where

**Every 7 days - 24 jurisdictions.** Arkansas, California, Colorado, Florida,
Georgia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Louisiana, Michigan, Mississippi,
Missouri, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Puerto Rico,
South Dakota, Tennessee, Virginia, Wisconsin and Wyoming publish their code in a
form that can be pulled whole - an official bulk export, an API, or a complete
per-section fetch - so every run re-pulls the entire code and reconciles it
against what is stored rather than crawling for what looks new.

<Note>
  **Colorado is checked weekly but re-pulled annually, and that is deliberate.**
  The Colorado Revised Statutes are published once a year, with no process for
  periodic updates, so Colorado's weekly slot re-pulls all 46 titles only when a
  new edition appears.
</Note>

<Note>
  The `cadence` field on `/statutes/coverage` and `/boards` reports `monthly`
  for every state statute board, these 24 included. Read it as the declared
  staleness window a board is alerted against, not as a ceiling on how often the
  corpus is re-pulled.
</Note>

**Everywhere else**, statutes are re-pulled on a monthly cycle or carry text as
of their ingest date, which is a fact you can read rather than one you have to
assume: `/statutes/coverage` returns `lastRetrievedAt` for every jurisdiction in
its `retrieval` array, and anything we have deliberately stopped re-pulling is
declared `paused` in `freshness` with its reason.

<Note>
  Pennsylvania's Consolidated Statutes are complete; its older unconsolidated
  (Purdon's) statutes are a separate later addition, prioritized for backfill.
  Constitutions and court rules roll out state by state.
</Note>

## How to query a jurisdiction

```bash
# Self-describing coverage matrix: every corpusType and its per-jurisdiction counts
curl https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/coverage \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."

# Live coverage with current section counts
curl https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/states \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."

# Statutory codes for one state (e.g. Mississippi)
curl "https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/codes?state=ms" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."

# Search within a state
curl -X POST https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/search \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..." \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"query": "landlord retaliation", "corpusType": "STATE", "state": "ms"}'

# Search state regulations (corpusType REGULATION, paired with a state)
curl -X POST https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/search \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..." \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"query": "stormwater discharge permit", "corpusType": "REGULATION", "state": "wa"}'
```

Each column in the matrix above maps to a `corpusType` filter on `/statutes/search`: statutes to `STATE`, regulations to `REGULATION`, constitutions to `STATE_CONSTITUTION`, and court rules to `STATE_RULES`. Federal Register agency rules use `FEDERAL_REGISTER` and agency guidance uses `AGENCY_GUIDANCE`. State agency guidance (the section above) uses `STATE_AGENCY_GUIDANCE`, paired with `state`.
