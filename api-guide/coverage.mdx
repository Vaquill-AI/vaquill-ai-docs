Every US jurisdiction and corpus type the API has ingested: federal law, state statutes, constitutions, court rules, and regulations, each scoped with the `corpusType` filter.

**Over 3.9M distinct sections, indexed as over 10.5M retrieval passages.** Figures below are a **2026-08-12** snapshot, except Oregon which is **2026-08-18**; query them live from `GET /statutes/coverage` and `GET /statutes/states`.

<Note>
  Counts here are **distinct sections**, one per provision or document, matching what `GET /statutes/coverage` returns. Longer documents split into several passages for retrieval, so the index holds more entries than sections, over half of them Federal Register rules at 13-15 passages each.

  That does not change what you get back: a citation lookup still resolves to one section with its full text.
</Note>

Every section is indexed for hybrid semantic + keyword search and mirrored in
multiple formats (HTML, PDF, XML, plain text, DOCX) where the source provides
them.

### Why nine jurisdictions show no court rules

Every gap is a source problem rather than a coverage decision, and each has a
different cause. Statutes, regulations and constitutions for these
jurisdictions are unaffected.

**[1] No government-published consolidated text** — Arkansas, Kentucky, New
Mexico, Vermont.
Each publishes its *amendment stream* (promulgation orders, rule changes,
proposed amendments) on the judiciary's own site, but licenses the consolidated
current text to a commercial publisher: Thomson Reuters for Kentucky,
LexisNexis for Vermont, and the Lexum "Norma" platform for Arkansas and New
Mexico. Vermont's judiciary says so in its own words, noting that all Vermont
court rules "are available through LexisNexis" and that it "does not administer
the LexisNexis website." Arkansas is the subtle one: its rules sit at
`opinions.arcourts.gov`, a `.gov` host, but the pages are the vendor's platform
served under the state's domain. The rule text is a government edict and in the
public domain; what is missing is a government-published compilation.

**[2] Only rule-change orders are published** — Colorado.
The judiciary posts adopted and proposed rule changes, not consolidated text.
The most recent government compilation is the Office of Legislative Legal
Services' 2023 edition, with no 2024, 2025 or 2026 successor. Ingesting a
three-year-old snapshot would serve superseded procedure as current law, so it
is deliberately not ingested.

**[3] Already served under `corpusType=STATE`** — Oklahoma, South Dakota.
Both codify their rules of court into the statute book (Okla. Stat. tit. 12;
SDCL tits. 15, 19 and 23A). Those rules are already in the corpus, cited the way
the state cites them (`SDCL 15-6-12(b)`). Ingesting them again under
`STATE_RULES` would be duplicate coverage, not new coverage.

**[4] Technical block** — New Jersey.
The judiciary publishes its rules as per-chapter PDFs, but `njcourts.gov` sits
behind a bot-protection layer that refuses those file paths. An engineering
problem rather than a sourcing or policy one, and it is queued.

**[5] Publisher policy** — Missouri.
Missouri prohibits automated access to its judicial websites under Court
Operating Rule 2.02, and its `robots.txt` opts out of AI crawlers. We honor
that. The route to Missouri coverage is a licence from the Office of State
Courts Administrator, not a workaround.

## Refresh cadence

How often each corpus is re-pulled from its official source. The counts above are
current as of the date at the top of this page and grow between updates; call
`/statutes/coverage` or `/statutes/states` for the live count of any
jurisdiction.

| Corpus | Cadence |
|---|---|
| Federal | See the [Federal table](#federal): daily to weekly, and on amendment for the Constitution. |
| State statutes | Two cadences, set by how the state publishes rather than by preference. **19 jurisdictions re-pull every 7 days** because their code can be pulled whole from the state's own publisher; the remaining scraper-crawled states re-pull monthly. Ten jurisdictions carry statutes that sit outside both drivers. See [Which statute cadence applies where](#which-statute-cadence-applies-where). |
| State regulations | 46 states carry regulations. **15 of them are on an automatic monthly refresh**; the rest were ingested as one-time cutovers and are not yet on a recurring driver, so their text is as of the ingest date. Call `/statutes/coverage` for what is present, and treat the refresh cadence as per-state rather than uniform. |
| State court rules | Monthly, for the states that carry them. Court rules change less often than statutes, and when they do the change is procedural and effective on a published date rather than immediately. |
| State constitutions | Monthly per jurisdiction, spread across the month rather than one pass (constitutions change on the order of years via ballot-measure amendments, not weeks, so this is deliberately lower-frequency than statutes/regulations). |

### Which statute cadence applies where

**Every 7 days — 19 jurisdictions.** California, Florida, Idaho, Illinois,
Indiana, Iowa, Louisiana, Michigan, Missouri, Nevada, New Jersey, New York,
Oregon, Pennsylvania, Puerto Rico, South Dakota, Virginia, Wisconsin and Wyoming
publish their code in a form that can be pulled whole — an official bulk export,
an API, or a complete per-section fetch — so every run re-pulls the entire code
and reconciles it against what is stored rather than crawling for what looks
new. Each holds a fixed slot in a rolling 7-day cycle and the dispatcher runs
daily, so the heavy ingests (California's full-code archive, Illinois's
per-section re-fetch) never land on the same day.

<Note>
  The `cadence` field on `/statutes/coverage` and `/boards` reports `monthly`
  for every state statute board, these 19 included. Read it as the declared
  staleness window a board is alerted against, not as a ceiling on how often the
  corpus is re-pulled.
</Note>

**Monthly — 19 states.** Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Connecticut, Kentucky, Maine,
Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North
Dakota, Ohio, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Texas, Washington and West Virginia
are crawled page by page off the legislature's own site. Each state has a fixed
day of the month, so the crawl spreads across the month rather than arriving as
one sweep. A quarterly deep pass re-fetches all 19 in full on a single day and
reconciles amended sections against what is already stored.

**No automatic driver — 10 jurisdictions.** Colorado, Delaware, the District of
Columbia, Hawaii, Kansas, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oklahoma, Utah and Vermont
serve complete, queryable statutes whose text is as of its ingest date, and the
reasons differ. Kansas's and Oklahoma's legislatures publish a `robots.txt` that
disallows automated fetching, so we stopped re-pulling them rather than route
around a published access policy; the text already ingested is the state's own
and stays live. Vermont's legislature blocks our requests outright. Colorado's
remaining fetch path reads a mirror rather than the state's own publisher, and
we re-ingest only from the publisher. Delaware, Hawaii and Utah need browser
automation that is not on a schedule. North Carolina and New Mexico have an
official bulk source but are run by hand, and DC's cutover is complete with its
recurring driver not yet wired. Colorado, Kansas, Oklahoma and Vermont are
declared `paused` in the `freshness` array on `/statutes/coverage`, each with
its reason; for the other six the `retrieval` array carries `lastRetrievedAt`,
so the age of the text is a fact you can read rather than one you have to
assume.

<Note>
  Pennsylvania's Consolidated Statutes are complete; its older unconsolidated
  (Purdon's) statutes are a separate later addition, prioritized for backfill.
  Constitutions and court rules roll out state by state.
</Note>

## How to query a jurisdiction

```bash
# Self-describing coverage matrix: every corpusType and its per-jurisdiction counts
curl https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/coverage \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."

# Live coverage with current section counts
curl https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/states \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."

# Statutory codes for one state (e.g. Mississippi)
curl "https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/codes?state=ms" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..."

# Search within a state
curl -X POST https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/search \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..." \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"query": "landlord retaliation", "corpusType": "STATE", "state": "ms"}'

# Search state regulations (corpusType REGULATION, paired with a state)
curl -X POST https://api.vaquill.ai/api/v1/us/statutes/search \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer vq_key_..." \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"query": "stormwater discharge permit", "corpusType": "REGULATION", "state": "wa"}'
```

Each column in the matrix above maps to a `corpusType` filter on `/statutes/search`: statutes to `STATE`, regulations to `REGULATION`, constitutions to `STATE_CONSTITUTION`, and court rules to `STATE_RULES`. Federal Register agency rules use `FEDERAL_REGISTER` and agency guidance uses `AGENCY_GUIDANCE`. State agency guidance (the section above) uses `STATE_AGENCY_GUIDANCE`, paired with `state`.
