`POST /us/statutes/search` accepts a terms-and-connectors expression when you send `matchType: "boolean"`.
It is the syntax used by the major legal research services: `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, same-sentence and same-paragraph connectors, numeric proximity, wildcards, exact phrases and grouping.

```json
{ "query": "\"trust fund\" AND penalty NOT 6672", "corpusType": "USC", "matchType": "boolean" }
```

Three guarantees hold for every Boolean search:

1. Every result satisfies the expression, judged over the section's full text rather than one excerpt or passage.
2. Results are ranked by relevance among the matches. The expression decides which sections qualify, and relevance decides their order.
3. Nothing is silently loosened or misread. A malformed expression, an unsupported operator or an out-of-bounds value is a `422` that names the problem and its character position.

## When to use Boolean

Use `boolean` when you need to say exactly which sections qualify and be told when your expression cannot be read.
Stay on `any` if you want the API to infer intent from a question.
[How search works](/api-guide/concepts/search#matchtype) compares every `matchType`.

For a citation such as `42 U.S.C. § 1983`, use `any` or `GET /us/statutes/resolve`.
A citation resolves to rank 1 in `any` mode, and Boolean mode never pins a cited section.

## Operators

Word operators are UPPERCASE.
Lowercase `and`, `or` and `not` are ordinary words.

| Operator | Matches a section where | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `AND` | both sides appear, anywhere in the section | `contract AND breach` |
| `OR` | either side appears | `work OR employment` |
| `NOT` | the left side matches and the right side appears nowhere in the section | `negligence NOT criminal` |
| `/p` | both sides appear in the same paragraph | `contract /p unconscionable` |
| `/s` | both sides appear in the same sentence | `covenant /s compete` |
| `/N` | both sides appear within N words of each other, in either order (N from 1 to 255) | `arbitration /5 award` |
| `+p` | same paragraph, and the left side comes first | `notice +p termination` |
| `+s` | same sentence, and the left side comes first | `notice +s termination` |
| `+N` | within N words, and the left side comes first | `notice +5 termination` |
| `atleastN(term)` | the term occurs at least N times in the section (N from 1 to 100) | `atleast5(negligence)` |
| `atmN(term)` | the term occurs at least once and at most N times (N from 1 to 99) | `atm2(negligence)` |
| `"exact phrase"` | these words, adjacent and in this order | `"summary judgment"` |
| `term1 term2` | bare words side by side are an exact phrase too | `summary judgment` |
| `*` or `!` at the end of a word | zero or more letters or digits | `retaliat*` |
| `?` inside a word | exactly one letter or digit | `wom?n` |
| `( )` | grouping | `(work OR employment) AND retaliation` |
| `caps(word)` | the word printed with at least one capital letter. `parsedQuery` lowercases the argument | `caps(IRS) AND penalty` |
| `allcaps(word)` | the word printed in all capitals | `allcaps(fdic)` |
| `nocaps(word)` | the word printed with no capitals, so "will" but not "Will" | `nocaps(will)` |
| `plural(word)` | the regular plural form, which `parsedQuery` shows as the plural word | `plural(deposit)` reads as `deposits` |
| `singular(word)` | exactly the word as written | `singular(fee)` reads as `fee` |

Details that matter in practice:

- Distance counts word positions. `/1` means adjacent, and `notice /3 termination` matches "notice of the termination" (three positions apart) but `/2` does not.
- `atleastN( )` and `atmN( )` take one term, phrase or wildcard: `atleast3("trust fund")`, `atleast2(retaliat*)`.
- Matching is on whole words, case-insensitively, with no stemming and no synonyms. `act` never matches `fact`, and `deposit` does not match `deposits` (see [Coming from another service](#coming-from-another-service) for the fix).
- Case functions read the text as printed, so `allcaps(irs)` matches "IRS" and not "Irs". `plural( )` adds a regular plural ending, so for an irregular plural type the plural word itself (`children`).
- Punctuation inside a word splits it. `§ 1983` is the phrase `section 1983`, and `rule 10b-5` is the phrase `rule 10b 5`.
- A hyphenated word on its own also matches its joined form. `non-compete` is read as `"non compete" OR noncompete`, and `at-will` as `"at will" OR atwill`.
- A slash between two words is text. `and/or` is the phrase `and or`, and `24/7` is `24 7`. Anything else containing `/`, `~` or `^` that is not a known operator is refused, never searched as words.

## Precedence

Without parentheses, operators bind in this order, tightest first:

1. `OR` (and `XOR`)
2. `/N` and `+N`
3. `/s` and `+s`
4. `/p` and `+p`
5. `AND`
6. `NOT`

Within one proximity level, the smaller distance binds first, and at an equal distance the ordered `+` form binds before the unordered `/` form.
Otherwise operators group left to right.

Legal research services use this order. Textbook Boolean binds `AND` tighter than `OR`.
`parsedQuery` parenthesizes every group whose reading could be in doubt, so you can read back exactly how your expression was grouped, and `queryWarnings` flags `AND` and `OR` mixed without parentheses.

| You send | `parsedQuery` | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| `work OR employment AND retaliation` | `(work OR employment) AND retaliation` | either word, and "retaliation" anywhere |
| `landlord /s tenant AND deposit` | `(landlord /s tenant) AND deposit` | the sentence pair, and "deposit" anywhere |
| `landlord NOT commercial OR industrial` | `landlord NOT (commercial OR industrial)` | "landlord", and neither excluded word anywhere |

<Accordion title="More precedence examples">

| You send | `parsedQuery` | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| `contract OR agreement /s breach` | `(contract OR agreement) /s breach` | either word, in a sentence with "breach" |
| `landlord /p tenant /s notice` | `landlord /p (tenant /s notice)` | "tenant" and "notice" in one sentence, inside a paragraph that also has "landlord" |
| `landlord /10 tenant /3 notice` | `landlord /10 (tenant /3 notice)` | the smaller distance binds first |
| `landlord /3 tenant /10 notice` | `(landlord /3 tenant) /10 notice` | the same rule, from the other side |
| `notice /5 termination +5 cause` | `notice /5 (termination +5 cause)` | at an equal distance, the ordered form binds first |
| `landlord AND tenant NOT commercial` | `landlord AND tenant NOT commercial` | both words, and "commercial" nowhere |
| `landlord NOT tenant /5 notice` | `landlord NOT (tenant /5 notice)` | "landlord", and no "tenant" within 5 words of "notice" |
| `landlord OR tenant /5 notice AND deposit NOT commercial` | `((landlord OR tenant) /5 notice) AND deposit NOT commercial` | all of the above at once |

</Accordion>

When in doubt, add parentheses.
They never change a correct expression, and `parsedQuery` drops the ones that are redundant.

Each side of a proximity connector (`/N`, `/s`, `/p` and their `+` forms) must be a term, phrase, wildcard, fuzzy term, an `OR` group of those, or another proximity expression.
`AND`, `NOT` and the counting functions cannot sit inside one: `(a AND b) /5 c` is a `422`.

## Also accepted

You can paste a search written in another service's syntax.
Each spelling below is read as the operator shown, and `parsedQuery` echoes the canonical form.

<Accordion title="Spellings from other services">

| Reads as | Spellings accepted | You send, then `parsedQuery` |
|---|---|---|
| `AND` | `&`, `&&` | `contract & breach` and `contract && breach` become `contract AND breach` |
| `OR` | `\|`, `\|\|` | `work \| employment` and `work \|\| employment` become `work OR employment` |
| `XOR` | `XOR`: one or the other, not both | `landlord XOR tenant` becomes `(landlord OR tenant) NOT (landlord AND tenant)` |
| `NOT` | `%`, a standalone `^`, `BUT NOT`, `AND NOT` | `negligence % criminal`, `negligence BUT NOT criminal` and `negligence AND NOT criminal` become `negligence NOT criminal`; `penalty ^ 6672` becomes `penalty NOT 6672` |
| `NOT` | a leading `-` on a word | `employer -retaliation` becomes `employer NOT retaliation` |
| nothing | a leading `+` or `#` changes nothing: every term is already required and exact | `+retaliation AND employer` becomes `retaliation AND employer`; `#retaliation` becomes `retaliation` |
| nothing | a boost such as `^2` does not change which sections match | `retaliation^2 AND employer` becomes `retaliation AND employer` |
| `/N` | `w/N`, `W/N`, `n/N`, `NEAR/N`, `AROUND(N)`, `NEARn`; bare `NEAR` is `/1` | `arbitration w/5 award`, `arbitration n/5 award` and `arbitration NEAR/5 award` become `arbitration /5 award`; `notice AROUND(4) termination` and `notice NEAR4 termination` become `notice /4 termination`; `notice NEAR termination` becomes `notice /1 termination` |
| `+N` | `pre/N`, `np/N`, `before/N`, `onear/N`, `ADJn`, `ONEARn`; bare `ADJ` is `+1` | `notice pre/5 termination`, `notice np/5 termination`, `notice before/5 termination` and `notice onear/5 termination` become `notice +5 termination`; `notice ADJ3 termination` becomes `notice +3 termination`; `notice ONEAR2 termination` becomes `notice +2 termination`; `notice ADJ termination` becomes `notice +1 termination` |
| `/s` | `w/s`, `w/sent`, `s/`, `WITH` | `covenant w/s compete` and `covenant w/sent compete` become `covenant /s compete`; `motion s/ denied` becomes `motion /s denied`; `notice WITH termination` becomes `notice /s termination` |
| `/p` | `w/p`, `w/para`, `p/`, `SAME` | `contract w/p unconscionable` and `contract w/para unconscionable` become `contract /p unconscionable`; `motion p/ denied` becomes `motion /p denied`; `notice SAME termination` becomes `notice /p termination` |
| `AND` | `w/seg`: a statute section is one segment | `landlord w/seg tenant` becomes `landlord AND tenant` |
| `NOT /N` | `NOT w/N`: negative proximity, see below | `landlord NOT w/5 tenant` becomes `landlord NOT /5 tenant` |
| `/N` or phrase | Phrase slop `"..."~N`: within N+1 words, any order. Slop 0 is the exact phrase | `"notice termination"~3` becomes `notice /4 termination`; `"notice termination"~0` becomes `"notice termination"` |
| `atleastN( )` | `atlN( )`. Function names are case-insensitive | `atl3(negligence)` and `ATLEAST3(negligence)` become `atleast3(negligence)` |
| `*` wildcard | `!` or `$` at the end of a word | `retaliat!` and `retaliat$` become `retaliat*` |
| fuzzy term | `~` alone is 2 edits. A similarity of 0.5 or more is 1 edit, below 0.5 is 2. See below | `negligence~` becomes `negligence~2`; `negligence~0.8` becomes `negligence~1` |
| spacing | missing or extra spaces around a connector are fine | `contract/s breach` becomes `contract /s breach`; `arbitration w/ 5 award` becomes `arbitration /5 award` |

</Accordion>

A `-` before a word is `NOT`, so `penalty -6672` excludes 6672 rather than searching for it.
A `!` before a word is refused: one family of services reads it as `NOT` and another as a wildcard, so we ask you to write `NOT criminal` or `criminal!` instead of guessing.

### Negative proximity

`NOT /N`, `NOT /s` and `NOT /p` (and `NOT w/N`) match a section where the left side occurs **at least once** outside that range of the right side.

`landlord NOT /s tenant` matches "The landlord sues. The tenant pays." and does not match "The landlord sues the tenant."
That differs from `landlord NOT (landlord /s tenant)`, which rejects the section the moment any one sentence holds both words.

### Refused, never searched as words

A token that looks like an operator is either understood or refused by name.
It is never quietly searched as ordinary words, because a misread operator returns a confident, wrong answer.
These are refused with a `422`:

- a leading `!` (`!criminal`), a doubled prefix (`--x`), and a double negation (`NOT -x`);
- a placeholder left in a connector (`w/n`), a connector we do not know (`s/5`), and any leftover `/`, `~` or `^` that is not one of the operators above;
- a distance in sentences or paragraphs (`WITH2`, `SAME3`): use `/s`, `/p` or `/N`;
- regular expressions (`/negligen.*/`), backslash escapes, number ranges (`1..5`) and ranges (`[2000 TO 2010]`);
- a case-sensitive phrase (`^"Internal Revenue"`): use the case functions in [Operators](#operators);
- `title:26`, which looks like a U.S. Code title number: use the `titleNumber` or `code` filters, or `cite( )`;
- any field a statute section does not have (see below).

The [Errors](#errors) table lists the exact message for each.

## Field restrictions

A term normally searches the section's body text.
Wrap it in a field function to search somewhere else.

| You send | `parsedQuery` | Searches |
|---|---|---|
| `title(security deposit)` | `title("security deposit")` | the section heading |
| `heading(security deposit) AND landlord` | `title("security deposit") AND landlord` | `heading( )`, `TI( )`, `HE( )`, `caption( )`, `name( )` and `title:` are the same field |
| `title:(lease OR rental)` | `title(lease OR rental)` | a group can go inside |
| `cite(1950.5) AND landlord` | `cite("1950 5") AND landlord` | the citation |
| `section(1983)` | `cite(1983)` | `section( )`, `CI( )`, `cite:` and `section:` are the same field |
| `text(landlord)` | `text(landlord)` | the body, which is what an unrestricted term searches |
| `unanno(landlord)` | `text(landlord)` | `TE( )`, `body( )`, `sd( )`, `unanno( )` and `text:` are the same field |

Write the function name directly before the parenthesis: `title (x)` is a `422`.
Fields a statute section does not have are refused by name rather than ignored: dates, judges, courts, parties and the other case-law fields, annotations, credits and history notes, and search-engine fields such as `site:`.
Filter by year with `yearFrom` and `yearTo`, by jurisdiction with `state`, and by code with `code` or `titleNumber`.
For a section's amendment history, read `GET /us/statutes/section/{actId}/changes`.

## Sentences and paragraphs in statute text

`/s` and `/p` depend on where a sentence and a paragraph end.
Statute text is not ordinary prose, so the boundaries are defined for it, and they are real boundaries rather than fixed word windows.

So in "(a) The landlord may recover. (b) The tenant shall pay.", `landlord /p tenant` does not match: `(b)` after a period starts a new paragraph.

<Accordion title="Where a sentence or paragraph ends">

A sentence ends at a sentence-ending period, question mark or exclamation mark followed by a new sentence.
Legal abbreviations, decimals and initials do not end one: "Sec.", "U.S.C." and "1950.5" stay inside their sentence.

A paragraph starts at any of these:

- a blank line;
- a subsection marker such as `(a)`, `(1)`, `(iv)`, `(A)` or `(aa)` that starts a line, or follows a `.`, `;` or `:`;
- a heading line starting `Sec.`, `Section`, `§`, `Rule` or `Article`.

A single line break is **never** a paragraph boundary, because much statute text is hard-wrapped mid-sentence.
A marker inside a sentence ("under subsection (b) of this section") does not start a paragraph, and a chained marker such as `(2) (a)` stays one paragraph rather than leaving the `(2)` alone.
A section with none of these boundaries is one paragraph.

</Accordion>

## Wildcards and fuzzy terms

`parsedQuery` echoes the expression unchanged except where the table says otherwise.

| You send | Matches |
|---|---|
| `retaliat*` | retaliate, retaliation, retaliatory, and "retaliat" itself |
| `wom?n` | woman, women |
| `1983*` | 1983, 1983a |
| `object$3` | "object" plus at most 3 more letters or digits: objects, objected, objection, but not objectionable. `parsedQuery` is `object OR object? OR object?? OR object???` |
| `object***` | the same: a run of 2 to 5 trailing `*` caps the extra characters, and `parsedQuery` is the same |
| `retaliat* claim` | a wildcard can sit inside a phrase. `parsedQuery` is `"retaliat* claim"` |
| `"landlord * deposit"` | `*` alone inside quotes is exactly one word: "landlord security deposit" |
| `negligence~1` | any word within 1 edit: "negligance" |
| `negligence~` | any word within 2 edits. `parsedQuery` is `negligence~2` |

Rules:

- A wildcard needs at least 3 letters or digits before the first `*`, `!`, `$` or `?`. `ab*` and `*ability` are refused. There are no leading wildcards.
- A length-limited wildcard (`$N`, or a run of `*`) allows 1 to 5 extra characters. Use a single `*` for any number.
- An expression can hold at most 20 wildcard and fuzzy terms, counting those inside phrases.
- A fuzzy term allows at most 2 edits (an insertion, deletion or substitution each) and needs at least 3 letters.
- A wildcard matches letters and digits only. It never crosses a space or punctuation.

## Text pasted from Word, PDFs and the web

The expression is normalized before it is read, so a search copied out of a document behaves like one typed by hand.

<Accordion title="What each pasted character is read as">

| Pasted | Read as |
|---|---|
| curly quotes, as in `“summary judgment”` | straight quotes: `"summary judgment"` |
| a minus sign or a typographic dash before a word | a leading `-`, so `NOT`: pasted as `trust` then a typographic dash right before `fund`, it is read as `trust -fund`, which is `trust NOT fund` |
| a non-breaking hyphen inside a word | a hyphen, so `"non compete" OR noncompete` |
| a curly apostrophe, as in `tenant’s rights` | punctuation, so `"tenant s rights"` |
| zero-width spaces and control characters | a space |
| fullwidth letters | ordinary letters |

</Accordion>

## Response fields

| Field | Present when | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `parsedQuery` | `matchType` is `boolean` | Your expression in canonical form: aliases replaced by the operators above, implicit phrases quoted, groups parenthesized, redundant parentheses dropped. Sending it back as the query runs the same search, so log, cache or show it |
| `queryWarnings` | `matchType` is `boolean` and the expression is valid but may not mean what you intended. Omitted when there is nothing to warn about | A list of plain-English strings. It never changes the search: the search ran as `parsedQuery` shows |
| `queryHint` | `matchType` is `any` and the query looks like a Boolean expression: an uppercase `AND`, `OR` or `NOT` between words, a proximity connector such as `/s` or `/5`, or `atleastN(`, in a query that would parse | The search ran as natural language, operators included as ordinary words. Retry with `matchType: "boolean"` if you meant the operators. A Boolean-looking query is never switched to Boolean mode for you, because the same request must mean the same thing every time |
| `totalMatches` | Boolean search. Absent only when the count could not be finished in time | How many sections satisfy the expression within your filters, across all pages. The same on every page of a query |
| `totalMatchesExact` | Boolean search | `true`: `totalMatches` is the exact number. `false`: it is an upper bound |
| `relevanceRankedCount` | Boolean search | How many leading results are ordered by relevance, at most 120. Results after them continue in keyword (BM25) order and repeat the last ranked `relevanceScore`, so you can reach every match without each one being re-scored |
| `nextOffset` | While `hasMore` is `true` | The `offset` to send for the next page |
| `matchContexts` | On each result, in Boolean mode only | Up to three short passages showing where that section satisfied the expression |
| `noGoodMatch` | Every search | `true` only when no section matched, and `false` otherwise. Every result matches by definition |
| `matchQuality` | Every search | `null` in Boolean mode. Its tiers are calibrated on natural-language ranking and do not describe membership in a Boolean set |
| `relevanceScore` | Each result | Orders the matches, as it does in every mode. Negated terms never contribute to ranking |
| `rankingDegraded` | Only when `true` | The order fell back to a simpler score. Every result still satisfies the expression |

<Accordion title="queryWarnings: the three cases">

| You send | `parsedQuery` | Warning about |
|---|---|---|
| `work OR employment AND retaliation` | `(work OR employment) AND retaliation` | `AND` (or `NOT`) and `OR` combined without parentheses. Services disagree on which binds first, so add parentheses to say which you mean |
| `landlord and tenant AND deposit` | `"landlord and tenant" AND deposit` | lowercase `and`, `or` or `not` next to real operators, searched as words |
| `retaliation^2 AND employer` | `retaliation AND employer` | a `^` boost, which was ignored because every result satisfies the expression and is ranked by relevance |

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="totalMatchesExact: when the count is only an upper bound">

`true` means `totalMatches` is the exact number of matching sections.
`false` means it is an upper bound: the expression uses an operator the count cannot judge exactly (phrases, `/s`, `/p`, proximity, `atleastN`, wildcards inside proximity), or you set a filter the count does not apply (anything other than `corpusType`, `state`, `actStatus` and `excludeRepealed`).
Every result you are served still satisfies the expression; only the count is a ceiling.
A section the corpus holds under two ids, such as a Federal Rule kept in two collections, is counted twice and served once.

</Accordion>

`nextOffset` is usually `offset` plus `limit`.
Past the relevance-ranked results a page can hold fewer rows than `limit`, because candidates outside your filters, or failing the expression on its full text, are left out.
When full-text verification runs out of time, the page stops early and `nextOffset` resumes exactly where it stopped, so always page with `nextOffset` rather than computing the offset yourself.

```python
offset, matches = 0, []
while offset is not None:
    page = requests.post(url, headers=h, json={**body, "limit": 50, "offset": offset}).json()
    matches += page["results"]
    offset = page.get("nextOffset") if page["hasMore"] else None
```

To get the total without the results, send the same expression to `POST /us/statutes/count` with `matchType: "boolean"`.
It returns the same figure as `count`, with `isExact` in place of `totalMatchesExact`, for 1 credit.

<Accordion title="matchContexts and highlights">

`matchContexts` shows where a section satisfied the expression, the way research services show hits in context.
Each passage is about 240 characters of the section's text, cut on word boundaries, with whitespace runs shown as one space and soft hyphens removed.
`highlights` lists `[start, end]` character offsets into that passage's `text` (end exclusive), so you choose how to style them.

The highlights come from the same engine that decides whether a section matches, so each one took part in the match:

- every conjunct of an `AND`;
- the `OR` branches that matched;
- a whole phrase;
- both operands of a satisfied `/N`, `/s` or `/p` pair (not the words between them);
- the occurrences `atleastN` counted;
- for `a NOT W/N b`, the `a` with no `b` in range.

A `NOT` never highlights anything, and a word that occurs only where it did not take part in the match is not highlighted.
The first occurrence of each part of the expression is shown before a second occurrence of any.
Offsets point at the text as printed, so a highlight for `section` can cover `§` and one for `café` can cover `CAFÉ`.

```python
for hit in page["results"]:
    for ctx in hit.get("matchContexts") or []:
        text, marked, last = ctx["text"], "", 0
        for start, end in ctx["highlights"]:
            marked += text[last:start] + "**" + text[start:end] + "**"
            last = end
        print(hit["citation"], marked + text[last:])
# Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5 the landlord shall **return** the **security deposit** within 21 days after...
```

Unlike `excerpt`, which is the passage the relevance model ranked and may not contain your terms, a context always does.
`matchContexts` is `[]` when the expression matched only in the heading or citation (`title( )` or `cite( )`).
It is absent on a row whose passages could not be prepared within the search's time allowance, such as a very long section; the row is still a verified match.
Contexts never change which sections are returned, their order or the price.
Leaving `matchContexts` out of `fields` skips the work entirely.

</Accordion>

## Recall: what you get and what you do not

Boolean mode guarantees precision: every section returned satisfies the expression.
It also pages to the end of the matches, up to the `offset` ceiling and the relevance-ranked count in [Limits](#limits).
Read `totalMatches` to know how many there are, and page with `nextOffset` until `hasMore` is `false`.
Four things still bound what comes back:

- The deepest offset is reached. When `totalMatches` is larger than you can page to, narrow with `state`, `corpusType`, `code` or `titleNumber`, or split the expression, to reach the rest.
- Wildcard and fuzzy terms can miss a rare word form.
- Very long sections can sit further down the pages than their relevance alone would put them.
- Verification time can stop a page early.

<Accordion title="Wildcard recall and verification time in detail">

**Wildcards.** Wildcard and fuzzy terms match the word forms found while searching.
`retaliat*` matches every form that turns up in the candidate sections, which in practice is every common form, but a rare form that appears only in sections the search never reached can be missed.
If one form matters, write it out: `retaliat* OR retaliatory`.

**Long sections.** When the words of an `AND` or a proximity pair sit far apart in a long section, that section is still found, but it can sit further down the pages than its relevance alone would put it.

**Verification time.** Multi-word phrases and proximity connectors are confirmed against each candidate's full text within one search's time budget.
When time runs out after some matches were confirmed, those matches are served as a normal `200`, and a candidate that was not confirmed is never served as a match.
When it runs out before any match was confirmed, the search is a `422` (see [Errors](#errors)).
Narrowing with `state` or `corpusType`, or using fewer `/s` and `/p` connectors, reaches the rest.

</Accordion>

An empty Boolean result means no section in your scope that the search reached satisfies the expression.
Before concluding the corpus holds nothing, loosen the expression (`/s` to `/p`, `/p` to `AND`, a phrase to its words joined by `AND`) or widen the scope.
To count the sections that satisfy an expression, send it to `POST /us/statutes/count` with `matchType: "boolean"`.
To count or list every section in a body of law regardless of wording, use `POST /us/statutes/count` without a query and `GET /us/statutes/divisions`.
See [How search works](/api-guide/concepts/search#when-search-is-the-wrong-tool).

## Pricing

A Boolean search is priced like any search, and every page is its own call.

<PriceTable />

A Boolean count on `POST /us/statutes/count` costs 1 credit, and a count of zero is refunded.
What is charged on a failure or an empty result is in [Failure and charging](#failure-and-charging).

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Query length | 500 characters |
| Terms, phrases and wildcards | 32 |
| Parenthesis nesting | 8 levels |
| Wildcard and fuzzy terms | 20 |
| Characters before a wildcard | at least 3 |
| Extra characters in `$N` or `***` | 1 to 5 |
| N in `/N` and `+N` | 1 to 255 |
| N in `atleastN( )` | 1 to 100 |
| N in `atmN( )` | 1 to 99 |
| Fuzzy edits | at most 2 |
| `offset` | 10,000 (70 in other modes) |
| Relevance-ranked results | 120 |

Exceeding any of the first eleven limits returns `422`.
Limits are refused, never truncated: a search that quietly dropped part of your expression would return a different set than the one you asked for.
Two limits behave differently:

- **Verification time** is one search's time budget. When no match was confirmed in time the search is a `422` and is not charged. Matches confirmed in time are served.
- **Relevance-ranked results** are not refused. Later results follow in keyword (BM25) order.

## Coming from another service

If you already write terms-and-connectors searches on another service, these differences matter:

| Habit | Here | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| A space means `OR` (or `AND`) | A space joins words into an **exact phrase**: `landlord tenant` is the phrase "landlord tenant" | `landlord OR tenant`, or `landlord AND tenant` |
| `*` is one character | `*` is **any number** of letters or digits, `?` is exactly one, and `***` caps the count | `wom?n` for one character |
| Lowercase connectors work | Word operators are **UPPERCASE** only. `landlord or tenant` is the three-word phrase "landlord or tenant", and `shall not` is the phrase it looks like | `landlord OR tenant` |
| Plurals and word forms are added for you | No stemming: `damage` does not match `damages` | `damage*`, or `damage OR damages` |
| `AND` binds tighter than `OR` | `OR` binds tightest | parentheses: `(a AND b) OR c` |
| `/s` and `/p` are word windows | They are real sentence and paragraph boundaries | `/N` for a word distance |
| `!word` excludes, or is a wildcard | A leading `!` is refused as ambiguous | `NOT word`, or `word!` |
| A word that looks like an operator is searched | An uppercase operator word is an operator | Quote it to search it as a word: `"OR" statutes` is read as `"or statutes"`, or write it in lowercase |

`AND` is always written out.
Two operands next to a parenthesis with no operator between them, as in `landlord tenant (deposit)`, are a `422` that names the missing operator.

## Errors

An expression we cannot read returns `422` with a `detail` string.
It names the problem and, where there is one, the **0-based character position** in your query.

```json
{ "detail": "Invalid Boolean query: Unclosed parenthesis at position 0" }
```

### Failure and charging

| Outcome | Cause | Charged | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| `422` | The expression could not be read. It is refused before any charge starts | No | Change the expression. The same expression returns the same `422` |
| `422` | The expression is too heavy to verify within one search. No match could be confirmed in time | No, refunded if a charge had started | Narrow it with `state` or `corpusType`, or use fewer `/s` and `/p` connectors, then send it again |
| `503` | The search backend is temporarily unavailable | No, refunded | Retry with backoff. Do not change the expression |
| `200` with no results | A valid expression that matches nothing is a completed search | Yes | Loosen the expression or widen the scope, see [Recall](#recall-what-you-get-and-what-you-do-not) |

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Unclosed quote or parenthesis | `"trust fund`, `(a OR b` | Close it |
| Missing operator before a parenthesis | `landlord tenant (deposit)` | Write `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, `/s`, `/p` or `/N` between the operands |
| Only a negation | `NOT foo` | Add a term to search for |
| Wildcard too short | `ab* x` | Put at least 3 letters or digits before the first wildcard character |
| Expression ends early | `a AND` | End with a term |
| Leading `!` | `employer AND !criminal` | Write `NOT criminal` to exclude, or `criminal!` as a wildcard |

<Accordion title="Why an expression can be too heavy to verify">

One `422` is about an expression's weight, not its syntax.
Every result is verified against the section's full text, and multi-word phrases and proximity (`/s`, `/p`, `/N`, `atleastN( )`, wildcards inside a proximity pair) need that check to confirm each candidate.
When the check runs out of time before confirming any match, the search returns `422` and is not charged:

```json
{ "detail": "This Boolean expression needs more time to verify than one search allows, so no result could be confirmed. Narrow it with `state` or `corpusType`, or use fewer sentence (/s) and paragraph (/p) connectors. You were not charged." }
```

</Accordion>

## For AI agents

Checklist for an expression that does what you mean on the first call:

1. Send `matchType: "boolean"`. Without it the operators are searched as words, and the response carries `queryHint` ([Response fields](#response-fields)).
2. Write operators in UPPERCASE: `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, `/s`, `/p`, `/5` ([Operators](#operators)).
3. Write `AND` between concepts, because a space makes a phrase ([Coming from another service](#coming-from-another-service)).
4. Parenthesize every `OR` group and any mix of operators, because precedence differs from textbook Boolean ([Precedence](#precedence)).
5. Read `parsedQuery` to confirm the grouping, log it rather than the raw string, and fix the expression any `queryWarnings` entry names ([Response fields](#response-fields)).
6. Page with `nextOffset` until `hasMore` is `false` ([Recall](#recall-what-you-get-and-what-you-do-not)).

On `422`, change the request before retrying.
On `503`, retry with backoff and leave the expression alone.
Both are covered in [Failure and charging](#failure-and-charging).

<Accordion title="More rules for agents">

1. **Quote multi-word terms** to make phrases explicit: `"security deposit"`.
2. **Prefer `/s` or `/p` to `AND`** when two ideas must be connected, not merely present in a long section.
3. **Every expression needs one positive term.** `NOT x` alone is refused.
4. **Scope with request fields, not query words.** Use `state`, `corpusType`, `code`, `titleNumber`, `yearFrom` and `yearTo`. Field functions exist only for `title( )`, `cite( )` and `text( )`.
5. **Quote `matchContexts`, not `excerpt`, to show why a section matched.** The highlights are the words that satisfied the expression; `excerpt` may not contain them. When `matchContexts` is absent, fetch the body before quoting.
6. **Read `totalMatches` first** when you need every match: when `totalMatchesExact` is `false` it is an upper bound. For the total alone, send the expression to `POST /us/statutes/count`.

</Accordion>
