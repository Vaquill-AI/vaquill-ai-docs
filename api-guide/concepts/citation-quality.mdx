---
title: "Citation resolution quality"
description: "How reliably GET and POST /us/statutes/resolve read citations, and which citations are refused on purpose."
---

`GET` and `POST /us/statutes/resolve` are built to refuse when they are unsure.
A missed citation costs a retry, but a wrong section can end up in a brief.

On our test set of 26,687 researched citations, 94.9% of the citations that should resolve returned the right section.
None returned a wrong one.

## What is refused on purpose

Some citations have no single correct section.
We refuse these, or answer and set `matchLevel` so you can tell, instead of guessing.

| What the citation names | Result |
|---|---|
| A chapter, article or part | Refused, or answered with its first section and a `matchLevel` |
| Several sections cited together | Answered with the first section and a `matchLevel` |
| A string that fits more than one law | Refused. Pass `state` |
| Law we do not hold | Refused |
| Something that is not a citation to U.S. primary law | Refused |

A bare rule-set abbreviation that several states print (`CR 56`, `RAP 2.5`) is refused unless you pass `state`.
See [Citation formats](/api-guide/concepts/citation-formats).

## Where coverage is thinner

- Court rules miss most often. Many resolve as soon as you pass `state`.
- Most district and county local rules outside those on the [coverage page](/api-guide/coverage) are not held.
- State session laws have their own endpoints and are not part of this resolver.
- State administrative registers resolve only where a held rule cites the register page.
