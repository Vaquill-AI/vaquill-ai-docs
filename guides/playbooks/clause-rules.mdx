One playbook holds one rule per clause type.
In Advanced detail level, each rule opens into three tabs.
This page is the reference for what every field does downstream, so you can decide which ones are worth filling in.

<Info>
  Only the preferred position is required, and even that becomes optional if you link a vetted clause instead.
  Everything else is depth you add where the negotiation actually happens.
</Info>

## Language

What the AI writes.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Linked clause from your library" icon="book-bookmark">
    Instead of describing the wording you want, link the exact clause.
    Search your [clause library](/guides/clause-library) by name, or create a new entry inline with a name and the clause text.

    When a clause is linked, drafting reproduces that text verbatim and adapts the surrounding section to it, rather than generating a paraphrase.
    The preferred position field then becomes optional notes, which is the right place for deviation rules or rationale that the clause text itself does not carry.

    Update the clause once in the library and every playbook that links it stays current.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Preferred position" icon="pen-nib">
    The clause wording you want by default. The AI drafts to this exact position.

    Write it as language, not as a wish.
    "Mutual cap at 12 months of fees paid, with a super-cap for data-breach liability" is actionable.
    "Reasonable liability cap" is not.

    An **Improve** action sits under the field and rewrites a rough note into a cleaner position statement, with an undo.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Acceptable range" icon="arrows-left-right">
    How far you will flex in negotiation.
    Give numeric ranges or alternative phrasings.
    Anything inside this range passes review without escalation.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Guardrails

The limits that trip review.

### Fallback ladder

An ordered list of acceptable retreats, best first.
When the preferred position is rejected, drafting takes the highest acceptable rung and review reports which rung a counterparty term actually lands on, instead of jumping straight to your worst case.

A three-rung liability ladder might read: 12 months of fees, then 24 months of fees, then total contract value.

This is the single highest-value optional field in a playbook.
It is what turns "we want X" into a negotiation you can delegate.

### Walk-away floor

The non-negotiable line for this clause.
A drafted or counterparty clause at or below it is forced to the most severe rating in review and to **Needs attention** in drafting, and it requires senior sign-off regardless of anything else.

<Warning>
  A walk-away floor with no fallback ladder makes the clause all or nothing.
  The [structure check](/guides/playbooks/structure-check) flags exactly that combination as a warning.
</Warning>

### Escalation triggers

Words or phrases that need sign-off if they appear.
The AI avoids them when drafting, and review flags them if a counterparty slips one in.
Keep them short and literal, for example "uncapped liability" or "no cure period".

### Numeric limits

Where a clause has typed constraints, the editor shows structured number fields instead of asking you to bury the number in prose.
These are checked deterministically against generated text, so they are far more reliable than the same figure written in a sentence.

| Clause | Numeric fields |
| --- | --- |
| Limitation of liability, Indemnification | Liability cap: unit (months of fees, fixed amount in USD, multiple of annual fees, or uncapped), the value, whether the cap is mutual, and which carve-outs sit outside it (confidentiality, IP infringement, data breach, willful misconduct, gross negligence, indemnification) |
| Termination | Cure period in calendar or business days; whether auto-renewal is permitted and the opt-out window in days; initial term in months |
| Payment terms | Net days, late fee as a percent per month, whether set-off is allowed |
| Data protection | Personal-data breach notification window in hours from confirmation |
| Non-compete | Duration in months post-termination |
| Confidentiality | Survival in years post-termination |

Clauses without applicable constraints show nothing here.

## Governance

Who signs off, and how loudly a miss is flagged.

### Priority

How negotiable the clause is.

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Unranked** | No signal |
| **Must-have** | Deal-critical. Raise first, hard to concede. Review surfaces these before the rest |
| **Should-have** | Strong preference |
| **Nice-to-have** | Concession candidate |

### Rationale

Why the firm holds this position: the precedent, the regulator concern, or the past deal that drove it. This is not decoration.

It is injected into the drafting prompt so the model can argue the position, and it is shown to reviewers in compliance results. The same **Improve** action is available here.

### Approval level on deviation

If a draft strays from this position, who has to approve before it can go out.

| Level | Who |
| --- | --- |
| **None** | Silent. Deviations are allowed without sign-off |
| **Manager** | Team lead |
| **Partner** | Senior counsel |
| **General Counsel** | Top authority |

### Conditional escalation

Raise the required sign-off automatically when the deal matches a rule.
Each rule reads as a sentence: if an attribute compares a certain way to a value, then require a given level of sign-off.

| Attribute | Comparisons | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deal value (USD)** | over, at least, under, at most | If deal value is over 1,000,000, require General Counsel sign-off |
| **This clause severity** | is, is not, is one of | Escalate when this clause comes back at the most severe rating |
| **Counterparty paper** | their paper, our paper | Require Partner sign-off whenever we are reviewing on their paper |
| **Governing law** | is, is not, is one of | Escalate when governing law is a state you do not normally accept |

Rules are evaluated at review time against the deal context you supply on the review form.
A rule that escalates to a level at or below the clause's base approval can never fire, and the structure check reports it as an error.

### Risk weight

How much a deviation on this clause matters, on a scale of 0 to 5.
It drives how loudly review flags the clause and how much it moves the overall compliance score.

| Weight | Label |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Boilerplate |
| 1 | Procedural |
| 2 | Standard procedural |
| 3 | Standard commercial |
| 4 | Important commercial |
| 5 | Financial or legal-existential |

Standard clause types come with a sensible default weight.
Setting a value overrides it, and the field shows both the default and your override so you can reset.

## Limitations

- Free-text fields are interpreted by a model. Precision in the wording is what makes enforcement precise.
- Numeric limits only exist for the clause types listed above. Everywhere else, a number has to live in the prose.
- Conditional escalation understands four attributes and nothing else. It cannot branch on counterparty identity, industry, region beyond governing law, or renewal status.
- A deal value threshold must be a plain number. Anything else is flagged as an invalid rule.
- Custom clause types carry no default risk weight and are not compared against the market-standard baseline.
- A linked library clause is reproduced verbatim, which means a stale clause in the library becomes a stale clause in every draft. Keep the library current.
- Escalation triggers match on language, so a counterparty who phrases the same concept differently will not trip them. Fallback ladders and numeric limits are the more robust guard.
