The Feed watches the topics you care about and surfaces new legal developments as they are published: regulatory moves, enforcement actions, notable decisions, and industry-specific legal news.

You tell it what to track. It checks continuously and keeps a per-item read state so you only see what you have not already dealt with.

## When to use it

- Following a regulator whose rulemaking affects your product
- Watching a counterparty, competitor, or acquisition target
- Keeping a practice area current without a daily manual search
- Tracking a statute or doctrine you expect to move

## Tracked interests

An **interest** is a topic you want watched. Add one and the feed starts discovering against it; delete it and the feed stops.

The app also **suggests interests** based on your existing work, which is usually a faster start than typing them cold.

<Tip>
  Interests are keyword-shaped, so be specific. "CCPA enforcement" surfaces far better material than "privacy". Narrow interests can be added freely, and a narrow one that goes quiet costs you nothing.
</Tip>

## Where the items come from

The feed is **deliberately web-only**, scoped per jurisdiction to an allow-list of authoritative legal domains: Cornell LII, Justia, congress.gov, Law360, Reuters, SCOTUSblog, and similar.

That is a considered choice rather than a limitation. A feed is about what is *new*, and Vaquill's own corpora are the wrong shape for it:

- The case-law mirror is a **periodic snapshot**, so nothing filed this week is in it yet
- The statutes corpus carries **year-granular** amendment data, not "this changed on Tuesday"

Both were surfacing stale material as though it were fresh, so the feed leans entirely on live web search. Statutory and case developments still appear, because the allow-listed sources publish them.

<Note>
  For the authoritative text of a statute or case, use [Statutes and Regulations](/guides/statutes-regulations) or [Case Law Research](/guides/case-law-research). The feed tells you something happened; the corpus tells you exactly what it says.
</Note>

## Working the feed

| Action | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Read** | Marks the item read and clears it from the unread count |
| **Dismiss** | Removes it from the feed without marking it read, for items that matched but do not matter |
| **Refresh** | Triggers a discovery pass rather than waiting for the scheduled one |

An unread count surfaces in the app so the feed is glanceable without opening it.

## Jurisdiction

The feed defaults to **US** sources and scopes discovery per jurisdiction. It is aligned with the rest of the platform: the corpus, the drafting defaults, and the research surfaces are all US-first.

## Limitations

- Items are third-party web content. Vaquill surfaces and dedupes them; it does not verify their accuracy.
- Coverage is bounded by the source allow-list. A development covered only by a niche outlet may not appear.
- It is not a docket alert or a compliance-monitoring service. Do not rely on it as your sole notice of a filing or a deadline.
- Published dates come from the source. Items missing one fall back to when Vaquill discovered them.
