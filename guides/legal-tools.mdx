Chat is open-ended. Legal Tools is the opposite: a small set of structured analyses where you fill in a short form, run it once, and get a consistently shaped result you can export, save, and hand to someone else.
Each tool asks for the same three things (a document, who you are in the deal, and any context worth knowing), then produces a graded, sectioned report rather than a paragraph of prose.

You reach the hub from **Review** in the sidebar. Every tool lives there as a tab, and your past runs sit in a history panel alongside them.

## The Tools

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Contract Review" icon="scale" href="/guides/contract-review">

  </Card>
  <Card title="NDA Triage" icon="file-shield" href="/guides/tools/nda-triage">

  <Card title="Risk Assessment" icon="triangle-exclamation" href="/guides/tools/risk-assessment">

  </Card>

## Running a Tool

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the tab">
    Open **Review** from the sidebar and choose a tool. Switching tabs keeps whatever you had typed in the other tools, so you can start a compliance check without losing a half-filled review form.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get the document in">
    Drag a file onto the upload area, browse for one, or pull it straight from Google Drive. Text is extracted before analysis, and scanned or image-only PDFs fall back to OCR automatically instead of failing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the framing fields">
    Contract type and which side you represent for Contract Review. Framework and document category for Compliance. These are what make the output take a position rather than describe the document neutrally.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add context, then sharpen it">
    Every tool has a free-text box (Review Instructions, Business Context, or Additional Context). Type a fragment such as "watch the liability stuff, we are the customer" and press **Improve** to expand it into an explicit checklist of issues before you run.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run and watch the steps">
    The run streams live progress ("Loading your playbook positions", "Flagging where it deviates") rather than a spinner, so you can see where a long document is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Act on the result">
    Export it, send the findings into a draft as anchored comments, or leave it in history and come back to it later.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Every Tool Shares

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Input handling and file formats" icon="file-arrow-up">
    Upload, paste, or import from Google Drive. PDF, Word, plain text, HTML, spreadsheets, presentations, and images are all accepted, and the extraction path is the same one the main document pipeline uses, so a scanned contract is run through the OCR ladder rather than rejected. Uploads are accepted up to 250 MB; the OCR tool caps at 100 MB and 1,000 pages. Analysis reads up to roughly 200,000 characters of document text, with a lower cap on the free tier.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Improve button" icon="wand-magic-sparkles">
    The context boxes are guidance notes, not questions and not documents, so they get their own rewriter. It expands scope, naming the clause types, carve-outs, and risk angles a competent lawyer would want covered, and it is explicitly barred from inventing facts. Party names, amounts, dates, citations, and jurisdictions you did not write come back as bracketed placeholders. Everything you did write, including which side you are on and any threshold you set, is preserved verbatim. If the rewrite fails, your original text is left untouched.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long documents are handled" icon="layer-group">
    Past roughly 12,000 characters, about four pages, a tool stops trying to read the whole document in one pass. It splits the document into sections, evaluates each one in parallel, then synthesizes a single report with a shared glossary of defined terms. This is why a hundred-page agreement completes instead of timing out, and it is also why a long document costs more and takes longer than a short one. Contract Review additionally picks its model by document length, so a three-page NDA does not run on the heavyweight model.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Grading vocabulary" icon="traffic-light">
    The tools deliberately share a color grammar. Green means acceptable with no action, yellow means negotiate, and red means escalate. Risk Assessment adds orange between yellow and red for its high band. Compliance uses Compliant, Partially Compliant, Non-Compliant, and Not Applicable instead, since a requirement is not a negotiating position.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Saved analyses and history" icon="clock-rotate-left">
    Every run is saved automatically and listed in the history panel next to the tool, newest first, with its grade. You can rename a run to something you will recognize later, delete it, or reopen it. Each saved run has its own link, so you can paste a specific review into an email or a matter note and it will open on that result.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Export" icon="file-export">
    Any result exports to PDF, Word, or Markdown, styled as a legal document rather than a raw dump. Contract Review has an extra export: the original contract with your accepted redlines applied as native Word tracked changes, which opens in Word with working Accept and Reject buttons.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Sending findings into a draft" icon="comments">
    Findings from a review can be pushed into a draft in the editor, where each one lands as a comment thread anchored to the clause it refers to. Findings whose language cannot be located in the draft are still attached rather than dropped, so nothing is silently lost.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Shared Vocabularies

The tools draw on the same taxonomies, which is what lets a playbook written for one contract type apply cleanly in review and what keeps clause names consistent between the clause library, playbooks, and a finished report.

| Vocabulary | Size | Coverage |
|------|------|------|
| Contract types | 39 | Commercial and vendor paper (SaaS, Master Services Agreement, Statement of Work, Reseller, Supply, Order Form), data and privacy (NDA, Data Processing Agreement, IP Assignment), employment (Offer Letter, Severance, Non-Compete), corporate and finance (Asset Purchase, Merger, SAFE, Term Sheet), and negotiated litigation paper (Settlement and Release, Stipulated Protective Order) |
| Your side of the deal | 20 | Commercial roles (Vendor, Customer, Licensor, Licensee, Reseller), employment (Employer, Employee), transactional (Buyer, Seller, Investor), lending (Lender, Borrower), confidentiality (Disclosing Party, Receiving Party), and litigation (Plaintiff, Defendant) |
| Clause types | 44 | The commercial core (Limitation of Liability, Indemnification, Termination, Confidentiality, Governing Law), plus specialized entries used by the deeper playbook templates such as Change of Control, Most Favoured Nation, Liquidated Damages, AI Training Data, and Release of Claims |
| Compliance frameworks | 14 fully supported | Privacy (GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA / CPRA, LGPD, DPDP, FERPA), sector rules (HIPAA, GLBA, TCPA, SOX), and security and resilience regimes (PCI DSS 4.0, SOC 2, DORA, NIS2) |
| Document categories for compliance | 13 | Privacy policies, Data Processing Agreements, terms of service, breach and incident plans, consent forms, vendor agreements, security policies, and financial reports |

<Tip>
  A framework is only "fully supported" if it has a real requirement checklist behind it. The picker will let you choose Other, but the result then falls back to generic reasoning and should be treated as a starting point rather than an assessment.
</Tip>

## Choosing Between Tools

| If you want to | Use |
|------|------|
| Know whether to sign a specific contract, clause by clause | [Contract Review](/guides/contract-review) |
| Clear a stack of inbound NDAs quickly | [NDA Triage](/guides/tools/nda-triage) |
| Score a situation that is not a document, such as a proposed business practice | [Risk Assessment](/guides/tools/risk-assessment) |
| Test a policy or agreement against a named regulation | [Compliance Check](/guides/compliance-check) |
| See what changed between two versions | [Compare Documents](/guides/document-comparison) |
| Ask the same questions across many documents at once | [Document Matrix](/guides/document-matrix) |
| Read text off a scan or a photograph | OCR, in the hub |
| Ask a one-off question about a document | [Chat](/guides/chat) with the document attached |

## Running Tools From Chat

You do not have to open the hub. With a document attached in chat, describing what you want routes the turn to the right engine, and the answer arrives inline with a card linking back to the full tool.

> *"Review this contract for risks"* runs a clause-by-clause review
>
> *"Should I sign this? Any red flags?"* runs a fast triage
>
> *"Is this compliant with GDPR?"* runs a compliance pass
>
> *"What are all my obligations and deadlines?"* sweeps duties and dates
>
> *"Compare these two versions"* offers a comparison rather than answering inline

Routing is conservative by design. Anything that would spawn a job, spend credits, or create a document is offered as a chip you confirm, not run silently. For a saved, repeatable version of one of these prompts, see [Skills](/guides/skills); for autonomous multi-step work, see [Agent Mode](/guides/agent-mode).

## Plans and Usage

Legal Tools are available on every authenticated plan, including free, but the shape of the limit differs.

- **Free**: five runs per tool per month, with a smaller document size ceiling of roughly twelve pages per run. The tab shows a used-of-total badge.
- **Paid plans**: no per-tool cap. Each run draws from your monthly message credits instead, and the tab shows the cost. Contract Review, NDA Triage, Risk Assessment, and Compliance Check are the expensive ones; OCR is the cheapest.
- Exporting a result, including the corrected Word export, is free. You already paid for the analysis.

See [Plans and Limits](/guides/plans-and-limits) for the full picture, or your billing settings for current usage.

## Limitations

- **The output is a first pass, not advice.** Every tool produces a structured opinion from the text you gave it and the framing you chose. It does not know your deal history, your risk appetite beyond what you typed, or anything outside the document.
- **Framing changes the answer.** Which side you say you are on drives the entire review. Getting it wrong produces a confident report arguing the other party's position.
- **A framework without a checklist is guesswork.** Fourteen compliance frameworks have real requirement lists. Choosing Other produces generic output.
- **Long documents lose some cross-references.** The section-by-section path shares a defined-terms glossary, but a contradiction between section 3 and section 41 is harder to catch than one inside a single clause.
- **Risk Assessment scores what you describe.** It reads your scenario, not your systems. A thin description yields a thin score.
- **OCR is not perfect on bad scans.** Faint photocopies, heavy stamps, and dense handwriting still lose characters. Check anything you intend to quote.
- **Some tool pages describe capabilities that live elsewhere.** Document Analyst is how document questions work in [Chat](/guides/chat); Redline Analysis runs through [Compare Documents](/guides/document-comparison); Contract Debugger and Canned Responses are not currently available as tabs in the hub.
- **Nothing here replaces review by a qualified lawyer** in the relevant jurisdiction.
